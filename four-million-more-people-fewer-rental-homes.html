Four Million More People. 26% Fewer Homes Available to Rent.

Britain's population has grown by nearly four million in a decade, but the number of properties available to rent has fallen by more than a quarter.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • 27 November 2026

More people. Fewer available homes. Higher rents.

Britain's housing crisis can be illustrated with three numbers.

  • 3,958,200 — the increase in the UK's population since 2016.
  • 26% — the reduction in homes available to rent.
  • 39% — the reduction in rental availability in London.

Yesterday, the Office for National Statistics revealed that the UK's population had reached 69,564,000, compared with 65,605,800 in 2016.

Meanwhile, research from Hamptons reveals just how much harder it has become to find a rental property.

Where are the homes?

England delivered just 203,690 net additional homes in 2025/26, fewer than in the previous year.

More than 1.3 million households are already waiting for social housing.

For those unable to afford a mortgage or obtain a council property, the private rental market is often their only realistic option.

Yet landlords face increasing taxation, tighter regulation and rising operating costs.

At the same time, immigration continues to contribute to population growth, even as annual net migration has fallen considerably from its peak.

Britain cannot solve its housing shortage without addressing both sides of the equation: the number of homes available and the number of households needing them.
