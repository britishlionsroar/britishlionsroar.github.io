Rapists, Paedophiles, Attempted Murderers: Every One of Them Can Be on a Flight Home After Serving a Fraction of Their Sentence. Only Terrorists Have to Stay.

Labour's sentence for foreign criminals is a taxpayer-funded ticket home to freedom. From 2027, many won't even have to serve a day of it first.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • November 2026
  • Under Labour's Early Removal Scheme, foreign prisoners can be deported after serving just 30% of the custodial part of their sentence.
  • The Prison Service's own rulebook bars only two groups from it: terrorists, and those serving life or indeterminate sentences. Rape, child sex offences and attempted murder are not barred.
  • The Government admits many foreign offenders could be deported after serving 10% of their sentence.
  • Homayon Ahmadi was jailed for 13 years for raping a woman he collected from hospital and sexually assaulting two more. He is already back in Afghanistan.
  • A British rapist is barred from Labour's new early release. A foreign rapist can still fly home early.
  • From 2027, eligible foreign criminals can be removed straight after sentencing, with no minimum time in prison at all.
  • Once deported, their sentence ends. Under Reform UK, foreign prisoners would serve their sentences abroad.
PURPOSE: To show, from the Government's own rulebook, legislation and statements, how Labour lets foreign rapists, sex offenders and violent criminals leave prison after a fraction of their sentence, how it plans to cut that to nothing, and what Reform UK would do instead.

Thirteen years.

That was the sentence handed to Homayon Ahmadi at Croydon Crown Court in March 2023, with a further seven years on extended licence.

Ahmadi came to Britain from Afghanistan in 2010 and was granted asylum. Britain gave him protection, then a licence to drive members of the public as a private hire driver.

On 19 April 2021 he collected a woman who had just been discharged from a hospital in Croydon. She was vulnerable and alone in the back of his car. He pulled into a garage and raped her.

Three months later, while police were still investigating the rape, he picked up two women from a nightclub in central London. When they tried to get out of his car in Hornsey Street, he grabbed them both and sexually assaulted them. When police arrested him, they found baby oil and condoms in his car.

He was found guilty of rape and two counts of sexual assault.

He is now back in Afghanistan. The Home Office has confirmed it. In 2023 the judge said thirteen years. This year he was on a flight home, and the rest of his sentence ended the moment the plane took off.

His three victims got a life sentence. He got a ticket home.

Sentenced to 13 years in 2023. Already back in Afghanistan.

The only foreign criminals who have to serve their time

Ahmadi is not a loophole. He is the system working exactly as designed.

The Early Removal Scheme lets foreign prisoners be taken out of prison and deported before they have served their time. Labour cut the minimum they must serve from 50% to 30% of the custodial part of their sentence, and stretched the window so they can go up to four years before their normal release date. Whichever point comes later applies. For most sentences that means 30%. For the longest, it means four years early.

So who is barred? The Prison Service's own rulebook, revised in March 2026, answers that question. The list is short:

  • Terrorists.
  • Those serving life or indeterminate sentences. Murderers stay only because murder carries an automatic life sentence.

That is it. There is no bar on rapists. No bar on paedophiles. No bar on attempted murderers, armed robbers, drug dealers or men who batter their wives. The rulebook even records that the old exclusion for sex offenders was scrapped back in November 2008, under Gordon Brown. Labour took the safeguard away then, and Labour has built on it ever since.

Prison governors can refuse an individual case on public safety grounds. That is a judgement call on paper, made prisoner by prisoner. It is not a ban on any crime.

Home by Christmas

If you think Ahmadi is a one-off, meet the surgeon.

A foreign national surgeon was jailed for seven years for sexually assaulting a patient in an NHS hospital, just 24 hours after her back operation. She could not move. She could not fight him off.

She has now been told by the authorities that, because he is a foreign national, he can be “released or removed from the UK” as early as 22 December 2026. That is roughly 13 months into a seven-year sentence. Home for Christmas.

The Ministry of Justice told LBC it “shares the public's anger”. It is Labour's Ministry of Justice. It is Labour's scheme.

One rule for British rapists, another for foreign ones

Here is the part Labour would rather you didn't notice.

Under Labour's new early release rules, most British prisoners on fixed sentences now walk out after serving a third. But after public fury, ministers excluded rape, serious child sex offences, grooming, manslaughter and other killings from that change. A British rapist has to serve his full custodial term.

Those exclusions do not touch the Early Removal Scheme running today. A foreign rapist is still eligible to fly home after 30% of his custodial term.

Commit the same crime, get the same sentence from the same judge, and the foreign criminal gets the better deal. Labour wrote both sets of rules.

Ten per cent

The judge says 13 years. The public hears 13 years. The victim is told 13 years. Then the arithmetic starts.

For most prisoners, only part of the sentence is ever served in custody. The Early Removal Scheme then takes just 30% of that part. Labour's own Ministry of Justice put it in writing in June 2025: combined with its other sentencing changes, many foreign offenders could be deported after serving 10% of their sentence.

Ten per cent. For an offence released at the one-third point, a ten-year sentence can become one year, followed by a flight home and freedom. Labour calls this saving taxpayers money. It reckons a prison place costs £54,000 a year. Apparently justice for a rape victim is worth less.

2027: zero

And Labour is not finished.

The Sentencing Act 2026 removes the minimum time foreign prisoners must serve before removal altogether. The Government has told Parliament that next year it will begin removing foreign offenders serving fixed sentences immediately after sentencing.

No 30%. No 10%. No prison time at all. And the four-year limit goes too: the Act says there will be no maximum period before an eligible offender can be removed. However long the sentence, the departure gate opens on the day it is passed.

After the outcry over rapists, ministers say the offences already excluded from their release changes will also be excluded from this one: rape, serious child sex and grooming offences, and unlawful killing. Those criminals fall back onto the 30% route instead, the route that sent Ahmadi home.

Everyone else can go straight from the dock to the departure lounge. Attempted murder, sexual assault, armed robbery, drug dealing, burglary: none of these appears on the Government's list of excluded offences. An attempted murderer handed an ordinary fixed-term sentence, which judges can and do pass, need not serve a single day before he is put on a plane.

First 50%. Then 30%. Then 10%. Now zero.

Come for the crime, leave on the next flight

For a career criminal who never meant to stay in Britain, prison is the only thing he fears. Deportation is not a punishment. It is a free trip home, the place he was going anyway.

Ion Zaharia and Samir Barbulesteanu drove off a ferry at Harwich and were burgling family homes in Cheshire within 24 hours. They ransacked children's bedrooms. They took £40 from a child's money box. Zaharia already had convictions across France, Austria and Romania. Police caught them at Birmingham Airport trying to board a flight to Amsterdam with cash and jewellery in their luggage.

They were jailed in January 2026 for four years and three months, and three and a half years. Under Labour's 30% rule, they could be on a plane after serving just a few months of it. From 2027, criminals like them may not serve a day.

Tell a burglary gang that the worst that happens if they are caught is a free flight home, and you are not deterring crime. You are advertising.

Deported, and back again

Labour's answer is that deported criminals are barred from coming back for life. Tell that to the people of Chester and Cambridgeshire.

Meltjan Isufi, an Albanian national, was deported after a drug supply conviction in 2019. He came back into Britain on a fake Greek identity card and set up a cocaine line in Chester with more than 800 customers. In October 2025 he was jailed for six years, including for knowingly entering the UK in breach of a deportation order.

Marian Cercel and Ionut Craciun were part of a gang linked to 27 burglaries across Cambridgeshire and Buckinghamshire, stealing jewellery worth more than £130,000. One raid on a home in central Peterborough netted £72,000 of gold. In August 2026 they were jailed at Cambridge Crown Court, having admitted knowingly entering the UK in breach of a deportation order.

The ban on returning is only as strong as the border behind it, and this is the same border that cannot stop the small boats.

Even getting them out in the first place is beyond the Home Office. Around 20,000 foreign offenders are living in British communities despite being subject to deportation orders.

Labour's ticket home versus Reform's cell abroad

Under Labour, deportation is the sentence. The foreign criminal leaves prison, gets on a plane and is free the moment he lands. The rest of his sentence simply disappears.

Reform UK would do it the other way round. Under its Keeping Criminals Behind Bars policy, launched in August by Nigel Farage and Reform UK Shadow Home Secretary Zia Yusuf, foreign prisoners would be transferred abroad to serve their sentences, either in their home countries or in rented prison space in countries such as Kosovo and El Salvador.

The ticket home would lead to a cell, not freedom. And possibly a cell far harsher than anything in Britain.

Removing foreign prisoners would free around 10,000 places in British jails. Reform would build “Nightingale prisons” on Ministry of Defence land, creating a further 12,000 places, so dangerous criminals are kept behind bars instead of being let out to make room.

Launching the policy, Zia Yusuf made a promise that no Labour minister has dared to make: “killers, rapists and violent thugs will never be released a day early”.

Labour gives foreign rapists a flight home and calls it justice. Reform would make them serve every day of their sentence and never let them back.

Homayon Ahmadi's victims are still living with what he did. He is living in Afghanistan, a free man. If you want that to stop, there is only one party offering to stop it.
