Ending the Asylum Scam


Summary

Reform UK has a comprehensive plan to fix Britain’s broken borders by stopping the boats
in the First 100 Days, and then detaining and deporting all illegal migrants and instituting
negative net migration. This paper outlines key additional measures to supplement Operation
Restoring Justice and Operation Fortress by ending the abuse of our generosity by economic
migrants who arrive illegally.

A Reform UK Government will end the Asylum Scam and:

•   Suspend the asylum system, voiding pending applications and marking new and current
    applicants for deportation.
•   Immediately close “resettlement” routes and reverse all refugee grants issued through
    these channels.
•   Introduce strict penalties for non-compliance with immigration law, including criminal
    liability for corporate executives profiting from illegal employment and substantial civil
    fines for landlords facilitating illegal housing.
•   Conduct a comprehensive national population audit using official records to identify,
    detain, and deport individuals residing in the country illegally. This could take the form
    of an emergency census. We will also be honest with the public about the full extent of
    migrant crime.
•   Mandate the immediate detention and deportation of foreign national offenders upon
    conviction, regardless of sentence length.
•   Embed immigration enforcement at all levels of local and national government by
    incorporating routine migration checks into standard regulatory inspections.
•   Protect the status of Ukrainians and BNOs, ensuring they only return once safe to do so.

Introduction


In Operation Restoring Justice and Operation Fortress, Reform UK set out the first steps we
would take in government to restore control to Britain’s borders. We have pledged to stop the
boats in the First 100 Days of a Reform Government. This document sets out further details
of our plan to end the asylum scam, end the invasion, and stop the Uniparty facilitating large
scale economic migration under the guise of humanitarian protection.

Our approach is simple. We will dismantle the pull factors drawing illegal migrants to Britain
and enriching unscrupulous employers, we will remove the barriers to deporting illegal
migrants and asylum applicants, and we will detain and deport illegal migrants, asylum
seekers and foreign criminals, while finally being honest with the British public about the
real costs of the asylum system.

To achieve this, we will begin by addressing the root cause of illegal migration: a dysfunctional
asylum system that has become a backdoor for economic migrants. We will suspend the
asylum system, and stop grants of refugee status or humanitarian protection in their entirety,
dismissing claims and deporting illegal migrants. Alongside this, our welfare reforms,
strengthened asylum detention, and a clampdown on illegal working will greatly reduce the
economic incentives to come to this country.

We have set out plans in Operation Fortress to intercept, detain and return those attempting
to enter Britain illegally by boat. We will also end the use of resettlement schemes, deport
those brought in through Labour’s new routes, reverse the Afghan resettlement schemes for
those who didn’t actually help our troops, and review previous grants of refugee status or
humanitarian protection while removing barriers to deportation.

We will put a stop to the migrant crimewave, strengthen laws on the deportation of foreign
criminals, and publish in full data on the nationality of offenders which the Labour Government
has sought to conceal. We will also audit the scale of the problem facing Britain, conducting a
full assessment of the size of the illegal population, and embedding immigration enforcement
throughout state functions.

In combination, these steps will make it impossible to enter Britain to claim asylum or live and
work illegally, and finally give the British public the truth about the failures of Uniparty policy.

The remainder of this document is set out as follows. Section one sets out our plans to
dismantle the pull factors bringing illegal migrants to Britain. Section two sets out our
plans to suspend the asylum system, end the Afghan ‘resettlement’ program, conduct a
full reassessment of refugee grants, and return illegal migrants to their home countries.
Section three sets out our plan to combat the migrant crimewave and conduct an audit of
illegal migration, and finally section four sets out plans to embed immigration enforcement
throughout the state.

Dismantling the pull factors


Perhaps the most important draw factor for illegal migration to the UK is the prospect of
ready access to welfare when claims are approved, and of illegal employment in the gig
economy while claims are assessed or even after they are declined. The Conservative and
Labour parties have turned a blind eye to the abuse of the British welfare state by illegal
migrants, and together have effectively decriminalised illegal working.

Under a Reform Government, this will change.

No access to welfare

As things stand, asylum applicants receive support and successful asylum seekers are granted
instant access to benefits. Government advice states that “as soon as you’re granted refugee
status, you should apply for benefits”.1

At present, there are over 130,000 refugees without British nationality claiming Universal
Credit, and an additional 52,000 people with grants of humanitarian protection.2 If their
share of Universal Credit claims is proportional to their share of non-British Universal Credit
claimants, this implies an annual cost to the taxpayer of almost £1.7bn in Universal Credit
alone.3 Given the disparities in incomes between Britain and the countries of origin of many
refugees, these payments are a powerful draw factor, particularly when combined with
access to the UK labour market and potential undeclared income.

Under Reform, the practice of enticing economic migrants with the prospect of humanitarian
protection and Universal Credit will end. Access to Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, taxpayer-
funded free childcare, and most other non-contributory benefits will be restricted to British
citizens. Asylum seekers and refugees, like other foreign nationals, would be denied access,
with exceptions for Ukrainians and BNOs.4

This bar to welfare access would include social housing. Almost 1 in 40 social homes let in
the last year of data were allocated to refugees.5 This is not an appropriate use of a limited
housing stock built at the expense of the British taxpayer. Reform UK will bring this to an
end.6

An end to illegal working

Under Reform UK, the practice of using illegal labour to lower wage bills will end.

We will pass the ‘Deliveroo’ Law making CEOs and Directors of large companies personally
and criminally liable for profiting from illegal working. The law will also impose fines of 10%
of global turnover levied against companies, and establish tip lines for the public to report
suspected illegal activity with follow-up enforcement mandated.7

To prevent illegal migrants from moving to smaller businesses, we will complement the
Deliveroo Law with a delegated power to issue penalties for illegal working to local councils
(in addition to existing enforcement powers). This will enable all council licensing, trading
standards, fire inspection and similar officers to do automatic immigration checks on their
visits. In the event that officers feel there is a reasonable suspicion of illegal working taking
place but there is a credible threat to council officers (in line with the widespread intimidation
of Trading Standards inspectors), they would also forward the case to the immigration
authorities.

Liability will be strictly subject to an adequate procedures defence, similar to that employed
in the Bribery Act. Councils would also be empowered to create additional licensing regimes
for frequent offender industries such as vape shops, corner shops, Turkish barbers, and
others. To prevent ‘phoenixing’ to remove liability, we would rapidly suspend licensing for
offenders via amending the Licensing Act 2003’s summary review process to include illegal

Operation Fortress: stopping the boats

In conjunction with the policies set out above, Reform UK will take active steps to combat

transport them back to the French or Belgian coast.

Every boat that is launched into the Channel will be detected, tracked, intercepted, and

Operation Fortress will be a lawful exercise of immigration powers under maritime law,

Emergency legislation will be used to give the Armed Forces the necessary immigration

Suspending the asylum system


A Reform Government will suspend the asylum system and grants of humanitarian protection
in their entirety.

We have already pledged that any migrants who entered the United Kingdom illegally,
overstayed a visa, or originated from a safe country will be ineligible for asylum. This would
effectively preclude any current asylum claimant from ever having their case heard, making
the asylum system superfluous.

A Reform Government will therefore suspend the asylum system entirely for a full parliamentary
term. All applications in train at the point of suspension will be voided. All applications made
during the period of suspension will be immediately declined, and the applicants marked
for deportation. No grants of humanitarian protection or refugee status will be made. Home
Office staff will be reprioritised to reviewing and overturning previous refugee grants.

As new cases will not be heard, and ongoing claims made by illegal migrants will be voided,
there will be no immigration and asylum tribunal claims. The tribunal has for too long acted as
a major block to deporting illegal migrants and the average time for asylum and humanitarian
protection appeals to clear the first-tier of the immigration tribunal currently stands at 72
weeks.11 There are currently 90,341 outstanding asylum and humanitarian protection appeals
within the tribunal system.12 Under a Reform Government these will not be heard and the
claimants will be deported. Outstanding European Economic Area (EEA) and EU Settlement
Scheme (EUSS) claims, and other immigration claims, will continue as internal Home Office
appeals subject to limited judicial review.

Ending “resettlement” schemes

In addition to asylum seekers arriving in the UK through illegal means or under false pretences,
under successive Labour and Conservative governments Britain became the second-largest
recipient of UN sponsored refugee flows in Europe.13 Some 481,804 people were brought to
the UK through “safe and legal routes” between 2015 and 2022.

The current Labour government has signalled its intent to greatly expand this flow, with
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood stating that she will reopen the UK Resettlement
Scheme immediately. Particularly large flows of Afghans and Palestinians are expected to
arrive without any democratic consent whatsoever.

Reform UK’s position on this matter is clear. There is no mandate for any such scheme. A
Reform Government will close them immediately. Anyone who has arrived in the UK through
these reopened routes prior to the election of a Reform Government will have their right to
remain revoked, and be deported to their country of origin or to a safe neighbouring country.

Alongside this step, we will undertake a full review of the Afghan resettlement schemes. The
Conservative Government expected “it would need to resettle around 4,000” Afghans, yet
chose to smuggle in 39,000.14 We will publish the full cost to the British public to date and the
full impact of their criminal behaviour. In addition, Reform UK will revoke the right to remain
of anyone that is not considered to be in any danger in their home country, and deport those
that have taken advantage of our country. Afghan veterans verified using MoD records will
be protected, although we consider that this number will be in the hundreds. Afghans will be
sent to a safe third country where it’s not appropriate to return them to Afghanistan.

review for current applicants.15 This does not go far enough. A Reform Government coming
into power in 2029 will extend the review to all those awarded grants of refugee status
or humanitarian protection in the preceding 5 years (this time limit does not apply to our

Ending the migrant crimewave


Detaining and deporting immigrant lawbreakers

The Home Secretary has the power to deport any immigrant whose presence in the UK is not
deemed as conducive to the public good.18 It is to the detriment of the British people that
this power is not fully utilised, often due to the ECHR.

Under the current rules, automatic deportations only apply if a foreign national receives a
custodial sentence of 12 months or more.19 It is evident that even this automatic deportation
category is ineffective, given that there are currently over 10,000 Foreign National Offenders
(FNOs) in British prisons, 12% of the total prison population.20 Further, EEA nationals with
settled status are currently exempt from automatic deportation despite their nationals being
amongst the highest represented FNOs in British prisons.21 The cost of FNOs has increased
by 50% since 2019 and they now cost the taxpayer £643m per year.22

Reform UK has already detailed plans for the routine removal of all FNOs given custodial
sentences over 12 months to serve their full term in third countries which offer prison spaces
for rental, including El Salvador and Kosovo. Under our policies, on completion of their
sentence these FNOs will be returned to their home country and handed a lifetime bar on
re-entry to the United Kingdom.

However, this policy will leave many repeat offenders off the hook. In 2025, 1.2 million
offenders were convicted across all criminal courts in England and Wales. Just 3% of those
convictions resulted in a custodial sentence of 12 months or more.23

There are 13 million immigrants living in Britain.24 Even if the rate of crime amongst foreign-
born populations were as low as among the British-born population, it would imply that
approximately 220,000 immigrants are convicted per year. If just 3% of these result in a
custodial sentence of 12 months or longer, then 200,000 non-British nationals are convicted
each year with a sentence which does not make them liable for automatic deportation. For
comparison, last year there were just 9,679 enforced deportations.25 There is no doubt that
there are hundreds of thousands of non-British nationals, walking free in Britain despite
having committed a crime.

It is important to note that the crimes in this category should not be considered as minor.
Shoplifting, assault, and possession of a knife are all crimes which routinely result in short
sentences.

Reform UK believes that a British visa is a privilege, not an entitlement. If, as an immigrant,
you violate the laws of the land, you should lose this privilege, and your presence be seen as
no longer conducive to the public good.

Reform UK will legislate, as part of the Illegal Migration (Mass Deportations) Bill, that
deportation must be considered during the sentencing of FNOs, with the judge or Home
Secretary handing down a deportation order. If non-British, upon conviction, regardless
of the sentence handed down, the individual’s visa will cease to be valid and they will be
detained and deported.

The operational decision regarding the deportation method will sit with Deportation
Command. The FNO will be detained until deportation.

Failure to count the illegal population

The last time the Home Office tried to count the illegal population was in 2005 when it
believed the number to be around 430,000.30 The National Audit Office confirmed in 2020
that the department “has not attempted to estimate the total illegal population since”.31
Twenty one years on since that count, more than 200,000 small boat arrivals and the largest
wave of legal migration in British history later, we still have no official updated estimate.

This issue is particularly pressing given the notable failure of successive governments to
track and match departure records against those of arrivals, creating a situation in which
visa overstayers can remain in Britain with little or no risk of enforcement action being taken.

At the end of June 2026 there were 93,293 asylum seekers receiving Home Office support,
16,021 of them in hotels.32 The Home Office selectively releases data. For example they have
told us that Glasgow holds 3,938 supported asylum seekers, Birmingham 2,136, Liverpool
1,961, Coventry 1,700 and Belfast 1,540, and that the North East and North West carry the
heaviest burden per head of population.33 In the year to June 2026, 33,374 people arrived
by small boat, and five nationalities, Eritrean, Sudanese, Afghan, Somali and Iranian,
accounted for two thirds of them.34 Given the lack of data for areas that the Home Office
itself acknowledges carry the heaviest burden, it appears that officials have been attempting
to conceal the true size of the disaster taking place in northern towns. Reform will put an end
to this and open the books.

Embedding immigration enforcement throughout the state


A Reform UK Government will direct every organ of the British state, both local and national,
to make it impossible to live in Britain illegally. This will involve several large policy shifts,
and many smaller changes. Overall, we expect it to produce a major increase in voluntary
departures.

Currently, Immigration Enforcement capacity is extremely weak. Just 12,791 illegal-working
visits were conducted in 2025.36 By contrast, there were 350,000 food hygiene visits,37 51,020
non-domestic fire safety audits,38 and 13,200 HSE workplace inspections.39 There were tens
of thousands more across trading standards, environmental health, housing and licensing
inspections.

Currently, these visits do not include an immigration check. But this could be easily incorporated.
Immigration checks do not require highly technical qualifications, and the officials that conduct
these visits should be trained to also conduct a basic migration check. This would increase
Immigration Enforcement capacity by a factor of at least 10, and up to 40.

These checks would feed data back to Deportation Command and the Migrant Identification
Centre, increasing their ability to identify illegal migrants, leading to a large increase in
voluntary and enforced departures. Revenue shared with local governments generated via
fines on those employing illegal workers will encourage local cooperation, which in turn will
generate more data for Deportation Command.

Housing

Reform UK will also end the use of hotels and dispersal housing HMOs. Homes will no
longer be leased to Home Office contractors such as Serco to house illegal migrants in local
communities. This will return at least 6,000 properties across the country back to private
use.40 We will further introduce new measures to crack down on landlords aiding and abetting
the invasion of Britain, preventing illegal migrants living outside of the purview of the state
from accessing private rented accommodation.

Local authorities currently issue civil penalties administratively for private sector rented
offences, up to £40k for continued breaches. We will issue Right to Rent penalties through
this mechanism, with a higher cap for repeated non-compliance. As with the above measures
on illegal working, a Bribery Act model of strict liability with an adequate procedures defence
would be used. If illegal migrants are found present on a property, the landlord will face
liability unless they could demonstrate they meet a high standard of procedures specified in
law (that would be sufficiently tight to render violations very rare).

Revenue from Right to Rent fines would not be earmarked for housing purposes and would
be available to councils for general expenditure. Unpaid charges will be levied as a local land
charge, accruing interest and collected on land sale. Current penalties are £5k/lodger and
£10k/occupier for a first breach, double for repeat offenders. These charges would be raised
considerably to a higher flat rate regardless of first time offences.

Detection will be possible via many channels, a number of which local councils are already
employing for similar purposes. Councils already hold council tax records and the electoral
roll, and can check this data against information supplied by the Home Office and Deportation
Command. “Invisible” illegal house-sharing and multiple occupation can be detected using data
on water, waste or electricity usage shared with them, aerial imaging, housing benefit registers
shared from DWP, complaints from neighbours or simply officers already on the premises for
some other violation such as noise complaints. Much of this is routine and has been employed
to different ends. In 2013, Slough Council, to crack down on “beds in sheds”, hired a thermal
imaging aircraft to map outbuildings to detect those that were likely inhabited.41

Even at a relatively modest detection rate of 10% of illegal occupiers per year, the fine
revenue from this would represent a significant boost to local authority funding, and further
incentivise cooperation.

Driving

Currently, there is too little attention given to catching illegal drivers, and a Reform
Government will stop turning a blind eye.

Driving licences will be set to automatically lapse on the end of immigration status (unless
that immigration is superseded by acquiring British citizenship or otherwise switching onto
another visa). The powers for this already exist under section 46 and 47 of the Immigration
Act 2014, which allow refusal and revocation of a licence on immigration grounds, which are
currently only executed on an ad hoc basis by the Home Office.

This will be combined with motor insurers being required to verify status before granting
insurance. As the Motor Insurance Database feeds automatic number plate recognition, this
would make illegal driving visible. Insurers persistently servicing illegal migrants would be
subject to penalties.

Those found to be driving illegally will have their vehicle seized. The large number of migrants
driving without insurance are doing so illegally, upon detection their visa will be revoked and
their deportation facilitated.

Companies House

Companies house does not currently verify immigration status at incorporation, we will
ensure that it does so. Likewise, sole-trader Unique Taxpayer References and Construction
Industry Scheme registrations will require identity checks to avoid illegal workers migrating
to these smaller schemes. This would also be re-verified upon registration for VAT and PAYE.

Cross-cutting immigration enforcement

Under the policies outlined above, every organ of the state will work to aid Deportation
Command in its role of deporting illegal migrants through co-enforcement and data sharing
with the Migrant Identification Centre. As enforcement on the part of local authorities will
deliver funding from fines levied, they will be incentivised to self-finance the build out of their
enforcement capacity, increasing state capacity even further. Every housing, food safety,
licensing, environmental health and trading standards inspection will become another part
of Operation Restoring Justice.

Data sharing with Deportation Command will be mandatory, providing the centre with
enormous inflows of data from inspections and enforcement. It will then send councils
verified cases involving employers, landlords, properties and businesses for them to enforce.
