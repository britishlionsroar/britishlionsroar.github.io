Rupert Lowe: Bad Memory or Two-Faced Liar?

No agreement with the Tories, he says. Except the part of the agreement he has already admitted to.

By Steve English, Independent British writer and editor · 7 November 2026

  • On 3 August Lowe told Dan Wootton: "There's no deal" and "There's no truth to the fact we have an agreement with the Tories."
  • On GB News he said he no longer calls the Conservatives "Lib Dem Tories" "as part of my agreement". The Telegraph's sources say silence was the price of his committee seat.
  • In the same Wootton interview he said his movement "served its purpose" because "the Tories put me on the Public Accounts Committee".
  • The Conservatives moved one of their own MPs off the committee to make room for him.
  • A former Tory cabinet minister and the Tories' London Assembly leader sat on Restore's advisory board. Both walked away as it became a party. Williamson's own register shows him leaving a week before it was registered.
  • Lowe's "offer" to Reform demanded that former Tory ministers be barred from any Reform cabinet. That means the Tories' defectors.

PURPOSE: To set Rupert Lowe's denials of any arrangement with the Conservatives against his own words on camera, the Conservatives' own actions, and the record of who Restore Britain fights and who it leaves alone.

On 6 November, Rupert Lowe posted a long statement on X. Reform politicians, he said, were spreading the idea that he was "somehow working in coordination with the Conservative Party and Kemi Badenoch". He likes Kemi personally. He speaks to her occasionally. He will "work with anyone in Parliament". And anyone who can't see that, he told us, isn't a "serious adult".

Fine. Let's be serious adults. Let's put Rupert Lowe's words next to Rupert Lowe's words.

No Agreement. Except the Agreement.

On 3 August 2026, the day after he publicly set out his terms to Nigel Farage, Lowe sat down with Dan Wootton. Wootton asked him straight out whether there was any sort of deal with the Conservative Party.

"Well, number one, there's no deal, Dan."

"There's no truth to the fact we have an agreement with the Tories."

Rupert Lowe, Dan Wootton Outspoken, 3 August 2026 (47:36 and 48:54)

No deal. No agreement. Nothing to see.

Now here he is on GB News, talking to Jacob Rees-Mogg about why he stopped calling the Conservatives "Lib Dem Tories":

"…as part of my agreement."

Rupert Lowe, GB News, with Jacob Rees-Mogg

So which is it, Rupert? There was no agreement, or there was an agreement and keeping your mouth shut about the Tories was part of it?

The Telegraph has an answer. On 3 August it reported that two sources said Lowe had agreed not to criticise the Conservatives during his committee appearances if the party nominated him to the Public Accounts Committee. In plain English: a seat in exchange for silence.

The Conservatives' response? "There is no deal between the Conservatives and Rupert Lowe – this is a figment of Mr Farage's imagination." Both sides deny the deal. One of them has already admitted it on television.

That leaves two possibilities. Either Rupert Lowe can't remember what he said on GB News, or he knows exactly what he said and is hoping you don't. I'll let you decide which is worse.

"It Served Its Purpose"

Earlier in that same Wootton interview, Lowe said something even more revealing. Asked about the Conservatives, he explained why he had set up his movement in the first place:

"The reason I set up a movement was to try and unite the right initially, and it served its purpose in that the Tories put me on the Public Accounts Committee… So look, it worked in that respect."

Rupert Lowe, Dan Wootton Outspoken, 3 August 2026 (25:35)

Read that again. The man who says he founded a party to fight the Conservatives describes a Conservative favour as proof that his movement worked.

Why Move Your Own Man?

In October 2025, the Conservatives handed Lowe one of their own seats on the Public Accounts Committee. Their stated reason was that he "shares our values on cutting waste". Later, a party spokesman added that "Kemi wanted someone with business experience on PAC".

To make room, the Conservatives moved one of their own MPs, Peter Fortune, off the committee. The official reason was to let him concentrate on the shadow science team.

Think about that. If the test was sharing Conservative values, the Tory benches are full of people who share Conservative values. They are called Conservative MPs. One of them already had the seat. The Tories took it off him and gave it to the leader of a party that says it wants to destroy them.

Nobody hands a rival a prize seat because he "shares their values". In my view they handed it over for something they wanted more. Lowe's own GB News admission tells us what part of that was: no more "Lib Dem Tories".

Tory Hands on the Foundations

Lowe's statement makes much of the fact that he left the Conservative Party in 1993. What he leaves out is who helped him build Restore Britain in 2025.

In July 2025, Lowe announced he was "absolutely delighted" to have Susan Hall, then leader of the Conservative group on the London Assembly, join Restore's advisory board. Sir Gavin Williamson, a serving Conservative MP and former Cabinet minister, joined the same board. His own entry in Parliament's register of interests records the role from 31 July 2025.

On 13 February 2026, Lowe announced that Restore would become a political party. Hall and Williamson both walked away. Williamson's register entry shows his role ending on 13 March 2026. Restore was registered with the Electoral Commission on 20 March 2026, one week later.

So for the whole period when Restore was being planned and built, senior Conservatives sat at the table. The moment it had to stand on its own two feet as a party, they stepped out of the photograph.

Then there's the phone. When Reform accused him of a Tory arrangement, Lowe told Wootton what he did about it:

"I've actually messaged Kemi and I've said, 'This is the accusation that Reform are making… do you have any issues that I'm unaware of?' And she said, 'No.'"

Rupert Lowe, Dan Wootton Outspoken, 3 August 2026 (48:22)

Political opponents don't message the other side's leader to check everything is still all right between them. Partners do.

Ban the Tories, But Only From Reform

This is where it stops being a contradiction and starts being a pattern.

On 2 August 2026, Lowe posted a 12-minute video offering Farage a pact on five conditions. One of them was this:

"Former Conservative ministers who failed our country and our people in so many important ways must not be part of any future cabinet. They had their chance."

Rupert Lowe, 2 August 2026

Who did that mean? Reform's former Conservative ministers, people like Robert Jenrick and Suella Braverman, with former Tory office holders such as Nadhim Zahawi and Jake Berry in Reform's senior ranks.

Now look at the double standard. Conservatives handed Lowe his committee seat. Conservatives sat on his advisory board. He "likes" the Conservative leader and checks in with her by text. All of that is fine.

But a Conservative who has left the Conservatives and joined Reform? Banned from government.

That isn't a man who dislikes Tories. It's a man who is very comfortable with the Tories who stayed, and wants the ones who left pushed out of Reform. Every defector who gives Reform ministerial experience is a defector the Conservatives lost. Lowe's condition would strip that experience out of a Reform government and make it look less ready to govern. Ask yourself who benefits from that. It isn't Reform.

The irony is hard to miss. In May 2025, Lowe had a private lunch in Mayfair with Robert Jenrick, then still a Conservative, and afterwards said he was "open" to joining the Tories. A Tory Jenrick was good enough for lunch. A Reform Jenrick must be kept out of the cabinet.

The minnow's demands

And look at who was making the demands.

1 MPRestore Britain. About 29 councillors. Control of no councils.
8 MPsReform UK. Around 2,300 councillors. Outright control of 24 councils.
3% v 21%Restore v Reform, YouGov, 1–2 November 2026

A one-MP party with a few dozen councillors made a public video telling a party with eight MPs, thousands of councillors and two dozen councils who it may and may not appoint to its cabinet. That isn't an offer. It's an ultimatum written to be refused, so that the man who wrote it can say he tried.

Farage refused that condition: "I don't ditch my friends." Within a day the "offer" had turned into a public row over a three-word text from Farage, "Will call later", with Lowe accusing him of "outright lying".

No meeting. No quiet approach. No private talks. A video, a list of demands and a fight. This is the same man who now complains that Reform ignores his emails.

The "False Allegations" Line

Lowe's statement claims Reform "tried to have me imprisoned over false allegations". Here's what the record actually says.

  • The police complaint. In May 2025 the CPS decided not to charge him over alleged threats to Reform chairman Zia Yusuf. Its reason: "insufficient evidence to provide a realistic prospect of conviction". Not enough evidence to prosecute is not the same as false.
  • The staff complaints. An independent investigation by Jacqueline Perry KC found "credible evidence of unlawful harassment of two women" by Lowe and male members of his team. Lowe disputes it and paid for his own KC to criticise how it was done.
  • The High Court. In May 2026 Lowe lost his High Court attempt to stop Parliament's own watchdog, the ICGS, investigating a complaint against him. It was his second defeat in that case. The judge ruled the challenge was barred by parliamentary privilege. It cleared nobody.

Shouting "false allegations" doesn't make any of that disappear.

Where Restore Stands, and Where It Doesn't

Lowe says Restore stands "everywhere and anywhere": in Reform seats, Labour seats and Tory seats.

On 29 October 2026, Reform lost Dudley's St James's ward to Labour by two votes. Restore took 159. The next day, former Tory MP Ben Bradley, who now leads Reform's local government wing, pointed out: "The only by-elections that Restore didn't stand in yesterday were the two Tory-held ones."

Durham County Council leader Andrew Husband put it more bluntly: "ResTory" stand when Reform is defending a seat but make "a habit of not standing when the Tories are defending a seat".

And Dudley wasn't a one-off. Here are six council by-elections since August:

The Army That Doesn't Vote

Restore cannot win. At the Makerfield by-election in June, its first parliamentary contest, it took 3,111 votes, fewer than the BNP managed in the same seat in 2010. YouGov's latest poll has it on 3%.

The online army behind it is not what it seems either. An analysis by Cyabra rated 39% of the profiles commenting on Lowe's X posts as fake. PoliticsHome and the Institute for Strategic Dialogue found pro-Lowe Facebook fan groups with tens of thousands of members run by admins in Pakistan, Bangladesh and New York. I set all of that out in 3,111 Votes, 1.3 Million Followers: The Fake Army Behind Rupert Lowe.

A party that can't win a seat doesn't need to. It only needs enough votes to stop Reform winning one.

Questions for Rupert Lowe

  1. You told Dan Wootton there was "no truth" in any agreement with the Tories. You told GB News you stopped saying "Lib Dem Tories" "as part of my agreement". Which one was true?
  2. What else was part of that agreement?
  3. Why did the Conservatives move one of their own MPs off the Public Accounts Committee to give you his seat?
  4. Why are former Conservative ministers acceptable as your advisers, but not as Reform ministers?
  5. Why were the only by-elections Restore skipped on 29 October the two Tory-held ones?

Far Too Cozy

Put it all together.

Senior Tories helped build Restore, then stepped away as it became a party. The Tories moved their own man off a top committee to give Lowe his seat. Lowe stopped calling them names "as part of my agreement", then told a national audience there was no agreement at all. He checks in with Kemi Badenoch by text when Reform asks questions. He demands Reform purge the ex-Tories who give it experience. And his party's votes keep landing in exactly the gaps that cost Reform seats, while Tory-held seats are left alone.

Rupert Lowe says he's the only grown-up in the room. In my view, he's the most useful man the Conservative Party has. Whether that's bad memory or two faces, I'll leave to you.
