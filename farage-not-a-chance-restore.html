Not a Chance: Farage Slams the Door on Restore's Jew-Deporters

Reform won't share a ballot paper with people who would drive Jews out of their own country. Rupert Lowe could fix that in an afternoon. He won't.

By Steve English, Independent British writer and editor · 24 November 2026

  • Over the weekend, an anonymous "senior Reform source" told the Guardian a "non-aggression pact" with Restore Britain was "in the air".
  • On Monday, Nigel Farage told the CBI conference: "I can't do a deal with people who want to deport all Jews."
  • On Tuesday, asked about Calvin Robinson and Steve Laws, he said "Not a chance". He said it twice.
  • On 13 November, Restore supporter Calvin Robinson said on a podcast: "I think driving out the Jews is the answer." Days earlier, he had spoken at a Restore donors' dinner.
  • Restore backer Steve Laws wants "all of them" deported, "even born here". Restore co-founder Charlie Downes suggested Farage had been bought by Jews.
  • Farage's condition hasn't changed since August: get rid of them. Lowe still hasn't.

PURPOSE: To set out exactly why Nigel Farage ruled out any pact with Restore Britain, who he was talking about, and what they have actually said.

For about forty-eight hours, the commentariat had it all worked out. Restore Britain had just won its first seat outside Rupert Lowe's own constituency: Michael Court took the Gunton ward by-election on Lowestoft Town Council. A "senior Reform source" told the Guardian that a "non-aggression pact" was "in the air" and there was a "deal to be done". The papers ran with it. The right was about to unite.

Then Nigel Farage opened his mouth, twice, and that was the end of that.

Monday: "I Won't Do a Deal With That"

Speaking at the Confederation of British Industry conference in London on Monday, Farage dismissed the talk of a pact. He didn't dress it up:

"I can't do a deal with people who want to deport all Jews. Let's just be frank. There are people in the upper echelons – in terms of support and profile – for Restore that want to deport every Jewish person out of Britain. I can't do a deal with that, and I won't do a deal with that."

Nigel Farage, CBI Conference, 23 November 2026, as reported by the Jewish Chronicle

He also told Lowe exactly how to change his mind. If Lowe removed those people, Farage said, it "would be a different kettle of fish".

Tuesday: "Not a Chance"

At his press conference in Westminster this morning, the question came again. Could he and Lowe "kiss and make up"? Farage said he "might have a civil chat" with him. But when he was asked about Calvin Robinson and Steve Laws, the answer was "Not a chance", and he said it twice. Lowe should disavow those people, he said, and Reform will not enter an electoral pact with an organisation that has people like them in senior roles.

Asked about the rise of ethno-nationalism on the online right, Farage named Robinson directly:

"There was the Calvin Robinson case, you know, not long ago, presenting a TV show in Britain, spoke at a Restore dinner two weeks ago, saying things in America for which I have no doubt he'll be kicked out of the country and never allowed back. So, yes, there is a rise. There is a rise in ethno-nationalism. And as you know very, very well, you know, I fought this all my career. No one did more to beat the BNP than me. And I really mean that."

Nigel Farage, press conference, 24 November 2026

That's not a man playing hard to get. It's a man drawing the same line he has drawn for over a quarter of a century.

Who He Means

Restore's defenders will tell you this is a smear. So let's look at the people Farage named, and let them speak for themselves.

Calvin Robinson

Calvin Robinson is the former GB News presenter and preacher. He posted in June that he had joined Restore. Earlier this month he spoke at an exclusive Restore event for donors. Restore's Cromwell Club asks for donations of at least £2,500. By his own account, he spoke about "the importance of Christian values" and said "we must stand up against white genocide". He then shared a photograph of himself with Rupert Lowe and Dan Wootton.

On 13 November, on The Command Show, the podcast he co-hosts with Joe Enders, he said this:

"I think driving out the Jews is the answer."

Calvin Robinson, The Command Show, 13 November 2026, as reported by the Jewish Chronicle

When it blew up, he didn't apologise. He doubled down on X: "Liberal Jews hold too much power & influence in Christian nations." And then: "We do not need to burn them. We can just deport them."

Read that last line again. That's the man who was on his feet at Restore's donors' dinner this month. There are now calls for the United States to deport him.

The Jewish Chronicle asked Restore whether Robinson's comments were compatible with party membership. Restore didn't answer.

Steve Laws

Steve Laws says he has "signed up and donated" to Restore. He founded Remigration Now, which calls for the expulsion of every non-white and Jewish person from Britain. In his own words: "When I say millions must go, I mean all of them. It's irrelevant whether they're illegal, legal or even born here."

Politico reported that when Farage warned in August about Restore supporters "demanding that all Jews be removed from the country", Laws was the man he meant. The Spectator reported that Lowe has refused to distance himself from him.

Charlie Downes

Charlie Downes is Restore's co-founder, spokesman and campaigns director. In February, Farage posted on X: "I can't be bullied. I can't be bought. I've stood for the same principles for many decades."

Downes replied with no words at all. Just a photograph of Farage speaking at the launch of the Reform Jewish Alliance, with a Star of David in the background.

No caption was needed. The message was that Farage had been bought by the Jews. It's the oldest smear in the book, and Downes posted it as a picture so nothing could be quoted back at him.

The Ball Has Been in Lowe's Court Since August

None of this is new. Farage set his condition in August, and it was simple: no talks while these people are in Restore's upper ranks.

Speaking at the Royal Horseguards Hotel on 10 August, he said there were "one or two people in the orbit of the upper echelons of that party who, if I sat down and talked to, I think our members and our voters would be utterly shocked that I'd done it". And then:

"Don't forget I have fought the far right for over a quarter of a century. I took on the BNP for years. It was a tough thing to do. But I won."

Nigel Farage, 10 August 2026, as reported by The Spectator

Lowe's answer, the very same morning, was to refuse:

"Farage and Reform have also said that Restore Britain must outright sack Charlie Downes – putting a young Christian man out of work, just as he is looking to build his life and start a family. That is simply not how I operate."

Rupert Lowe on X, 10 August 2026

Three and a half months on, Downes is still there. Laws is still welcome. And Robinson is now telling the world Jews should be driven out, with Restore's leader beside him in the photographs.

Farage hasn't moved the goalposts. He has left them exactly where they were. Lowe is the one who keeps refusing to kick the ball.

Reform Locks the Door. Restore Leaves It Open.

Reform has a written list of banned organisations: the BNP, the National Front, Patriotic Alternative, the Homeland Party and more. Anyone on it is out. When Reform found BNP members on its candidate lists this year, it threw them out, even with their names already printed on the ballot paper. I set out that rulebook in full in BNP 2.0? "Welcome to the Team, Mate".

Restore has no such list. It has a donors' club that pays to hear a man talk about "white genocide".

And here's the difference that matters to every Reform voter. Reform will deport illegal migrants and foreign criminals. That's the policy, in black and white. Steve Laws wants to deport British citizens, "even born here". Calvin Robinson wants to drive out Jews. One of those is border control. The other is the BNP with a new logo.

Questions for Rupert Lowe

  1. Calvin Robinson said "driving out the Jews is the answer". Is he still a member of Restore Britain?
  2. Why did Restore not answer when the Jewish Chronicle asked whether his comments are compatible with membership?
  3. Do you still welcome the support and money of Steve Laws, who wants people deported "even born here"?
  4. Charlie Downes is still your spokesman. What did his picture of Farage at the Reform Jewish Alliance mean, if not "bought by the Jews"?
  5. Farage has told you what it takes to talk. Why is keeping these men worth more to you than beating Labour?

Only One Party for the Patriotic Right

Restore can't win a general election, and its own leader knows it. What it can do is split the vote just enough to hand seats to Labour. That's been the pattern in by-election after by-election.

Now it's asking Reform voters to hold their noses and do a deal with people who want Jews driven out of Britain. Farage's answer was the right one, and it was short: not a chance.
