The Accusation, From His Own Mouth

Starmer Called It a Squalid Chapter. He Is Still Writing It.

On 12 May 2025, Keir Starmer stood before the cameras and delivered what was billed as a defining moment for his government. The occasion was the publication of the Immigration White Paper. The tone was one of a man who had finally identified a national scandal and intended to end it. The words he chose were striking.

“This strategy will finally take back control of our borders and close the book on a squalid chapter for our politics, our economy, and our country.”

— Keir Starmer, Immigration White Paper Press Conference, 12 May 2025

A squalid chapter. His words. Not ours. He went further.

“When you have an immigration system that seems almost designed to permit abuse, that encourages some businesses to bring in lower-paid workers rather than invest in our young people, or simply one that is sold by politicians to the British people on an entirely false premise, then you're not championing growth, you're not championing justice — you're actually contributing to the forces that are slowly pulling our country apart.”

— Keir Starmer, 12 May 2025

A system designed to permit abuse. Sold on an entirely false premise. Contributing to forces pulling the country apart. Again, his words. And then this:

“We do have to ask why parts of our economy seem almost addicted to importing cheap labour rather than investing in the skills of people who are here and want a good job in their community. Sectors like engineering, where visas have rocketed while apprenticeships have plummeted. Is that fair to Britain? Is it fair to young people… to miss out on those apprenticeships, to see colleges in their community almost entirely dedicated to one-year courses for overseas students? No, I don't think it is. And truth be told, I don't think anyone does. And yet that is the Britain this broken system has created.”

— Keir Starmer, 12 May 2025

The Britain this broken system has created. The Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, on the record, in a prepared speech to the nation, describing his country's immigration system as broken, abusive, false-premised and destructive.

Now look at what his government's own Skilled Worker visa eligible occupations list — live, published, and marked eligible on GOV.UK today, more than a year after that speech — actually says about who Britain needs to import.

Read it carefully. Because it is one of the most damning documents in modern British political history. And it belongs entirely to this government.

The Government's Own List

The Roles Britain Apparently Cannot Fill Without Importing People From Abroad

The following roles are taken directly from the UK government's current Skilled Worker visa eligible occupations list, published at GOV.UK. Every single one of them is marked eligible for international sponsorship as of June 2026 — after the White Paper, after Starmer's speech, after every promise of reform.

We have grouped them not alphabetically or by SOC code, but by the nature of the outrage they represent.

Category One

Roles the British State Should Never Outsource to Foreign Nationals

These are not peripheral roles. They are the organs of the British state itself. They require intimate knowledge of British law, British culture, British communities, and British civic life. The idea that they should be filled by people imported from abroad is not merely questionable — in several cases it is constitutionally dangerous.

Let us be direct about what this means. A foreign national, sponsored on a Skilled Worker visa, can be appointed as a senior police officer commanding British forces. They can sit as a judge in a British court. They can issue British passports. They can run British elections. They can be the officer who decides whether your benefits claim is valid or whether your house meets planning standards.

At what point does a government stop merely failing its people and start actively hollowing out the institutions that define a nation?

Category Two

Building Parallel Societies at Taxpayer Expense

The second cluster of roles on the eligible list is, in its own way, even more damaging than the first — because the damage it does is slower, quieter, and by the time it is visible, it is already structural.

When a newly arrived community's social worker speaks their language, their children's teaching assistant shares their cultural background, their housing officer comes from the same country of origin, their youth worker operates within the same religious framework, their counsellor, their care worker, their debt adviser, their community support worker — when every point of contact that community has with British public services is staffed by someone who mirrors rather than integrates them — you have not created a multicultural society. You have created a parallel one.

And the British taxpayer has funded its construction.

This is not multiculturalism. Multiculturalism, as a functioning social model, requires people of different origins to engage with one another and with the shared civic framework of the country they have joined. What the eligible occupations list enables instead is the wholesale importation of the administrative apparatus of a community from the country of origin — so that the community itself never needs to engage with Britain at all.

The integration never happens, because the system actively prevents it. And then politicians stand at podiums and express bewilderment that communities feel like islands unto themselves.

Category Three

Stealing Jobs From British Workers While Paying Them to Stay Home

There are 1.81 million unemployed people in the United Kingdom. That figure represents a 5.0% national unemployment rate. Within that number, an estimated 110,000 to 120,000 are Muslim — people who, according to the argument used to justify halal-specific visa sponsorship, are uniquely qualified for roles in halal food businesses. They are already here. They already have the right to work. They already understand halal food standards. There is no shortage.

And yet the eligible occupations list includes chefs, bakers, butchers, fishmongers, catering managers and takeaway proprietors — all sponsorable from abroad.

But it goes far beyond food. The list includes:

27:1Non-EU young migrants hired for every young British national since 2020
355%Rise in non-EU under-25s on UK payrolls since 2020
<1%Growth in young British nationals on payrolls over the same period
730,000Young British people aged 16–24 currently NEET (not in employment, education or training)

Between December 2024 and December 2025, the number of non-EU workers aged under 25 on UK payrolls increased by 33,200. Over the same twelve months, the number of UK nationals of the same age fell by 32,200. Almost person for person. Almost pound for pound of wage going to a foreign national instead of a British young person.

These are not high-skill, degree-level positions being filled by world-leading specialists. These are starter jobs in retail and hospitality — the exact roles that have traditionally been the first rung on the employment ladder for British school and university leavers. Roles they are now being locked out of, not by their own failure, but by a system that makes it cheaper to hire a foreign national than to invest in a British citizen.

“We do have to ask why parts of our economy seem almost addicted to importing cheap labour rather than investing in the skills of people who are here and want a good job in their community.”

— Keir Starmer, 12 May 2025. The irony is without parallel.

The trades and manual roles on the eligible list — bricklayers, roofers, carpenters, electricians, plumbers, painters, plasterers, welders, vehicle mechanics — deserve particular scrutiny. These are not arcane specialisms. They are the backbone of the British building and maintenance economy. They are also precisely the roles that a properly funded domestic apprenticeship programme would fill. And they are roles that the Conservative governments before Labour systematically defunded and neglected, allowing the shortages that justified the sponsorships in the first place.

The cycle is deliberate, even if no single government set out to design it that way. Defund apprenticeships. Create a shortage. Use the shortage to justify importing workers. Make imported workers cheaper than domestic ones through salary discounts and fee exemptions. Watch British young people fall out of the labour market. Repeat.

The Backdoor Still Open

How Vape Shops and Car Washes Are Still Importing Workers Using Management Codes

The government raised the general salary threshold to £41,700 and removed hospitality and catering roles from the standard Skilled Worker route from 22 July 2025. This was presented as closing the backdoor that allowed kebab shops, takeaways and similar businesses to sponsor kitchen workers under the generic Chef designation (SOC Code 5434).

It did not close the backdoor. It moved the door.

A car wash cannot legally sponsor someone under "Car Washer." A vape shop cannot sponsor a "Shop Assistant." But both can — and examples have been documented — sponsor individuals under the following codes, which remain fully eligible on the current list:

Management Codes Being Exploited by Small Businesses
1150
Managers and Directors in Retail and WholesaleIntended for high-level corporate retail management. Currently being used to sponsor "store managers" for individual vape shops and corner stores.
1131
Financial Managers and DirectorsStandard salary threshold: £75,100. Cash-heavy independent businesses can meet this threshold on paper to secure visas for associates or family members — regardless of whether the business genuinely warrants a corporate finance director.
1132
Marketing and Sales DirectorsUsed by small import/export and wholesale businesses — including vape distribution operations — to claim they require international expertise for brand management and foreign supply chains.
1133

1258
Directors in Consultancy ServicesOne of the most elastic codes on the list — "consultancy" is sufficiently vague to encompass almost any business activity, making it highly vulnerable to misclassification.
1259
Security Services Managers, Recruitment Agency Managers, Cleaning and Hygiene Services ManagersAll eligible. All verifiably used by small independent operators to bring in workers who would not qualify under any other code.

The Home Office knows about this. Media coverage, parliamentary questions and the government's own audit findings have all identified the pattern. The codes remain open.

And the halal staffing argument — the claim that a halal-certified food business requires workers with specialist religious food preparation skills that cannot be found domestically — has been comprehensively dismantled by the logical chain that Gemini AI, of all things, was eventually forced to concede under sustained questioning: a business is either halal certified or it is not. A halal-certified business does not handle non-halal meat. There is therefore no cross-contamination risk to manage. There is no "halal skill" that cannot be found among the 110,000 to 120,000 unemployed Muslims already residing in the UK. The number of halal-certified UK food businesses that sell non-halal meat is zero. The justification for halal-specific visa sponsorship is, equally, zero.

It is the official position of the United Kingdom that these roles face shortages so acute, so beyond the capacity of the domestic workforce to fill, that they must be recruited internationally.

Keir Starmer said the system was sold to the British people on an entirely false premise.

He was right. And his government is still selling it.
