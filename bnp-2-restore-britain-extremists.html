BNP 2.0? “Welcome to the Team, Mate”: The Extremists Reform Bans and Restore Britain Welcomes

Reform UK's constitution bars former members of the BNP, the National Front and Patriotic Alternative. Rupert Lowe's party is signing them up.

By Steve English, Independent British writer and editor · 5 November 2026

Who's on Restore's side of the door

  • A self-declared Nazi
  • A man jailed for stirring up racial hatred
  • A campaigner who wants British-born citizens deported
  • A white supremacist banned from Britain
  • Former BNP men
  • Reform UK's constitution revokes the membership of anyone found to be a current or former member of a proscribed organisation. The BNP, the National Front and Patriotic Alternative are all on the list.
  • Rupert Lowe replied "Welcome to the team, mate" to a former Patriotic Alternative organiser who calls himself a "Nazi-Buddhist".
  • A man jailed for stirring up racial hatred has joined Restore and says he wants to be one of its MPs.
  • A campaigner who wants every non-white person removed, "even born here", says he signed up and donated.
  • An American white supremacist banned from Britain is a paid-up member.
  • Asked whether any of them had been expelled, Lowe called the reporting "pointless smear pieces. Absolutely pathetic."

PURPOSE: To test Restore Britain against the rule Reform UK already enforces on itself, using the party's own constitution, Lowe's own words and published reporting. The question is whether the people Reform shuts out have found a new home.

Rupert Lowe likes to tell voters he is the real thing: harder on immigration than Reform, and braver than Farage. So start with what Reform actually does about extremists, and then look at what Restore does.

The Rulebook

Reform UK's constitution is blunt. Under clause 4.8.4, anyone "later found already to be a member or former member of another political party or organisation membership of which the Board has declared to be incompatible with membership of the Party" has their membership revoked. It is mandatory, not a matter of discretion. The constitution says the Party Chairman "shall revoke" it.

Reform's list of proscribed organisations names twelve groups. Among them are the British National Party, the National Front, Patriotic Alternative, the Homeland Party, the British Democrats, For Britain and National Action. Candidates sign a declaration that they have never belonged to the BNP. Nigel Farage has called it "a golden rule". When Reform found candidates on a leaked BNP membership list before May's local elections, it expelled them, three in April and two more in London in May, even though their names were already printed on the ballot paper.

You can think what you like about Reform. But it has a door, and the door is locked.

Restore Britain has no such list.

Welcome to the Team

Chris Mitchell, from Gorleston in Lowe's own constituency, was Patriotic Alternative's organiser for the East of England. He calls himself a "Nazi-Buddhist". In one message, reported by the Eastern Daily Press, he describes himself as "a racist, misogynistic, anti-semitic, islamophobic homophobe".

His posts are worse than his description of himself. Byline Times reported that he wrote that "Jewish control over our people WILL be coming to an end". Searchlight reported that on his Telegram channel he wrote: "forgive me if I don't for one second believe six million died during the holocaust". On 20 April, Hitler's birthday, he posted: "Happy birthday Unc." He renamed that channel "Restore Britain War Room".

On 1 December 2025, Mitchell shared Lowe's announcement of Great Yarmouth First, Restore's flagship local group, with the words: "finally something we desperately needed. Sign me up!"

Lowe's account replied: "Welcome to the team, mate."

As reported by the Eastern Daily Press, 3 March 2026

Mitchell went on to leaflet for the party and posed for a selfie with Lowe at a Great Yarmouth First event. The EDP reported that an account run by Lowe's own office manager, also a Great Yarmouth First candidate, interacted with him online. Mitchell claims that when he asked Lowe whether his views were a problem, Lowe told him: "post what you want, I am for free speech." Lowe has never addressed that claim.

When the story broke, Lowe called it "a concerted media smear campaign" and said Restore was "not responsible for policing the opinions of random members of the public".

He had welcomed him to the team three months earlier.

Under clause 4.8.4 of Reform's constitution, a former Patriotic Alternative organiser would be barred from membership outright.

The Members Reform Would Never Take

Mitchell is not a one-off. On the eve of the Makerfield by-election, the Telegraph named a string of extremists who had joined or funded Restore. Three stand out.

Sam Melia was jailed for two years in 2024 for stirring up racial hatred. He was an active supporter of National Action, now banned as a terrorist group, and is a leading member of Patriotic Alternative. In March he announced he had joined Restore and said he would like to stand as one of its MPs.

Steve Laws says he has "signed up and donated" to Restore. He founded Remigration Now, which calls for the expulsion of every non-white and Jewish person from Britain, and leads a faction that split from the Homeland Party, which is also on Reform's list. He put it this way: "When I say millions must go, I mean all of them. It's irrelevant whether they're illegal, legal or even born here."

Jared Taylor, the American founder of the white supremacist magazine American Renaissance, has been banned from entering Britain since 2015. He is a paid-up member. He told his followers: "You can be a member even if you are banned from Britain, as I am!"

Under Reform's rules, Melia's Patriotic Alternative membership would bar him. Laws's "even born here" is the opposite of everything Reform stands for. Taylor isn't allowed into the country.

The Old Guard

The BNP veterans have found their way in too. Eddy Butler, who ran the BNP's election campaigns in East London and once challenged for its leadership, turned up at the founding meeting of Restore's Epping Forest branch. Julian Leppert, a former BNP councillor now with the British Democrats, has publicly endorsed the party. Michael Newland, a former BNP official, handed out Restore leaflets in the Holborn and St Pancras by-election.

In Makerfield, the Observer published a photograph of Restore's candidate, Rebecca Shepherd, smiling with a group of campaigners. One of them was a member of Patriotic Alternative.

Every one of them would fail Reform's membership rules.

Lowe's Answer

To be fair to Lowe, he has said publicly that he detests neo-Nazism. But words cost nothing, and a party is judged by what it does.

When the Telegraph asked Restore whether any of these men had been expelled, Lowe did not say yes. He did not say no. He said:

"The Telegraph has failed to cover the findings of our rape gang inquiry report… yet finds the time to publish such pointless smear pieces. Absolutely pathetic."

Rupert Lowe, to the Telegraph, June 2026

Reform found BNP members on its candidate lists and threw them out within days. Restore was handed the names of a jailed racist, a Holocaust denier and a man who wants British-born citizens deported, and its leader changed the subject.

Who Holds the Door Open

None of these people can win an election, and neither can Restore. In Makerfield, the party polled 3,111 votes, fewer than the BNP's 3,229 in the same seat in 2010.

But it doesn't need to win. In Dudley in October, Reform lost a council seat to Labour by two votes, with Restore on 159. In six council by-elections since August, Restore's vote was bigger than Reform's losing margin every time. A Times MRP poll suggests that Restore standing everywhere would cut Reform from 108 projected seats to 59. The full figures are in my earlier article, 3,111 Votes, 1.3 Million Followers: The Fake Army Behind Rupert Lowe.

So every voter tempted by Restore should ask one question. Reform built a door and locked it against the BNP, the National Front and Patriotic Alternative. Rupert Lowe opened his and said "welcome to the team".

Which party really takes extremism seriously, and who is that open door really helping?
