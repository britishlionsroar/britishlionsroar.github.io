PURPOSE: To show, in plain figures, that 305 years of government by the same three political families has produced the debt, the housing crisis, the borders and the broken promises Britain lives with today — and that for the first time in that entire history, voters have a genuine alternative.

In April 1721, Robert Walpole was appointed First Lord of the Treasury. He is generally regarded as Britain's first Prime Minister. Every Prime Minister since — through wars, recessions, wartime coalitions and dozens of general elections — has come from one of three political traditions. The Whigs became the Liberals. The Tories became the Conservatives. Labour joined them in government two centuries later, when Ramsay MacDonald formed the first Labour government in 1924. Names changed. Parties split, merged and re-formed. Politicians crossed the floor. Coalitions were built and dissolved. But control of the country never once left that closed circle.

Generation after generation was raised to treat its vote as inheritance rather than choice. "Our family votes Labour." "This is a Conservative area." "My father voted for them, and his father did before him." It barely mattered what any of them promised, delivered, or failed to deliver. Red blamed blue. Blue blamed red. Power was handed back and forth within the same establishment, election after election, generation after generation.

They have had 305 years to get it right.

Look at what that 305 years has produced. Public sector net debt stood at 95.1% of GDP at the end of May 2026 — levels last seen in the early 1960s. The OBR's own forecast has the tax burden reaching a post-war high of 38.5% of GDP by 2030-31. The median home in England now costs 7.6 times the median full-time worker's salary, a burden that has locked an entire generation out of ownership. In the year ending December 2025, 813,000 people made a long-term move to the UK — and even as net migration has fallen sharply from its 2023 peak, the figure remains historically enormous by any standard these parties promised voters for decades they would control. Alongside that, 43,806 people were detected arriving through illegal routes in the year to March 2026, 90% of them on small boats, at record average boat sizes. None of this happened under one government. It happened under successive governments from all three of the established parties, each promising the last one had failed and each ultimately presiding over the same trajectory.

Look, too, at what is happening in Westminster right now. Keir Starmer announced his resignation as Prime Minister on 22 June 2026, with the formal handover to Burnham due on 20 July, days after Labour MP Josh Simons stood aside specifically to open a parliamentary seat for Andy Burnham. Burnham won that by-election, entered the Commons, and became the only candidate to secure enough nominations for the Labour leadership. He is being installed as Prime Minister without a general election, and without Labour's own membership casting a single vote in a leadership ballot. Britain did not vote for an Andy Burnham government. Even the voters of Makerfield were asked only to elect a constituency MP, not a Prime Minister. This is what "handing power back to another branch of the same establishment" looks like when it happens in real time, not as history.

Clacton tells a similar story from a different angle. Nigel Farage triggered a by-election there on 7 July 2026 while facing a parliamentary investigation into whether a £5 million private gift, given to him by Christopher Harborne in June 2024 before he became an MP, should have been declared once he took his seat. He said he wanted his own constituents to judge him rather than the Standards Commissioner. Whatever you make of his reasons, Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats and the Greens all confirmed they will not field a candidate. At the 2024 general election, those four parties won more than 24,000 votes between them in Clacton. This time, not one of those voters will have their chosen party on the ballot at all. Whatever their leaders think of Nigel Farage, why should their own members and voters be denied a candidate? They can call the election a circus. Their voters still lose their choice.

For the first time in 305 years, that is starting to change.

NatCen's British Social Attitudes survey states plainly: Reform UK has been ahead in the opinion polls throughout the last twelve months, and no party outside Labour and the Conservatives has ever previously held that position. YouGov's most recent poll, 12–13 July 2026, put Reform on 24%, five points clear of the Conservatives and Labour, both on 19%. This is not a brief protest surge of the kind British politics has seen before and watched fade. It is the first sustained national lead by an outsider party in modern British polling history — and, for the first time in the entire 305-year run of this monopoly, an outside political force now has a credible route to government.

They will tell you that voting Reform is a gamble. But after 305 years of debt, unaffordable housing, uncontrolled borders and broken promises from the same three political families, the real insanity is expecting a different result from the same establishment, one more time.

This is the chance to break the circle.
