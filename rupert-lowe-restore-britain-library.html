DEAL OR NO DEAL, RUPERT?

Here's where Rupert Lowe's story gets interesting.

The Conservatives surrendered one of their allocated places on Parliament's powerful Public Accounts Committee.

Rupert Lowe got it.

Lowe was subsequently asked whether his place on the committee resulted from a deal with the Conservatives.

His answer?

“NO, THERE WAS NO DEAL.”

Clear enough.

Except Rupert Lowe has also publicly said:

“I PROMISED NOT TO CALL THEM THE LIB DEM TORIES AS PART OF MY AGREEMENT.”

Now we have a problem.

“NO DEAL.”

But also:

“MY AGREEMENT.”

And Lowe's wording tells us something else.

He didn't say that WAS the agreement.

He said it was:

“PART OF MY AGREEMENT.”

In other words, stopping the “Lib Dem Tories” attacks was one part of a wider agreement.

So:

WHAT WAS THE REST OF RUPERT LOWE'S DEAL WITH THE TORIES?

We know the Conservatives surrendered one of their allocated committee places.

Rupert got the seat.

We know Rupert made at least one commitment to the Conservatives.

He promised to stop calling them “Lib Dem Tories”.

But Rupert's own words tell us that was only PART of his agreement.

What else did Rupert agree to?

What else did the Conservatives agree to?

What else did Rupert get?

What else did the Conservatives get?

And the most obvious question of all:

WAS THE PUBLIC ACCOUNTS COMMITTEE SEAT PART OF THAT AGREEMENT?

Because the public shouldn't have to guess.

Rupert Lowe knows what was in his agreement.

The Conservatives know what was in it.

SO PUBLISH THE WHOLE THING.

DEAL OR NO DEAL, RUPERT?

Rupert Lowe likes to portray Restore Britain as the alternative to Britain's failed political establishment.

There's just one slightly awkward chapter in the origin story.

THE TORIES HELPED HIM BUILD IT.

When Restore was still developing as a political movement, its advisory board included Sir Gavin Williamson — former Conservative Defence Secretary, Education Secretary and Chief Whip.

Alongside him was Susan Hall.

Not an ex-Tory.

AN ACTUAL SERVING CONSERVATIVE POLITICIAN.

Hall was a Conservative London Assembly member and leader of the Conservative group at City Hall.

So Rupert was apparently perfectly happy getting into bed with senior Conservatives while Restore was being established.

Then Restore became a registered political party.

And suddenly?

THANKS VERY MUCH, TORIES.

WE'LL TAKE IT FROM HERE.

Reporting at the time said Williamson and Hall were expected to end their involvement once Restore became a political party.

Now Rupert lectures Reform about former Conservatives.

A former Tory Cabinet minister helping build Restore?

Apparently fine.

A serving Conservative politician advising Restore?

Apparently fine.

But a former Conservative who actually leaves the Tories and joins Reform?

NOW RUPERT HAS A PROBLEM.

HOW MUCH OF RESTORE WAS BUILT WITH HELP FROM THE VERY TORIES RUPERT NOW TELLS REFORM SUPPORTERS NOT TO TRUST?
