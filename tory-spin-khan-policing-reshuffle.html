Sacked? Forced? The Conservative Spin on Khan's Policing Reshuffle

The City Hall Conservatives say their "tough questioning" forced Sadiq Khan to sack his policing deputy. She wasn't sacked. Khan gave no reason. And the Tories were far from the only ones asking.

By Steve English, Independent British writer and editor · November 2026

  • Kaya Comer-Schwartz was not sacked. Sadiq Khan's own announcement names her London's new Skills Commissioner. Even the Tories' "SACKED" graphic says "demoted" in the small print.
  • Khan gave no reason for the move. The BBC's London political editor offered four possible explanations. Conservative questioning was not one of them.
  • The Conservatives' video promising the "full exchange" contains no questioning at all, just a 28-second piece to camera.
  • Reform UK's London Assembly leader, Alex Wilson, was pressing Comer-Schwartz and the Mayor from July 2025 on shoplifting, police front desks, climate spending and the vigilantes filling the gaps on London's streets.
PURPOSE: To test the City Hall Conservatives' claim, in their own words, against the Mayor's announcement and the London Assembly record, and to set out the questions Reform UK has been putting to City Hall on London's policing.

On Thursday 19 November 2026, Sadiq Khan reshuffled his deputy mayors. Lib Peck, director of London's Violence Reduction Unit, is to become Deputy Mayor for Policing and Crime, subject to confirmation by the London Assembly. Kaya Comer-Schwartz, who had held that job for two years, becomes London's new Skills Commissioner. [Mayor of London]

The same day, the City Hall Conservatives put out their version. On Facebook:

"BREAKING NEWS. Sadiq Khan has sacked Kaya Comer-Schwartz from her role as Deputy Mayor for Policing and Crime."City Hall Conservatives, Facebook, 19 November 2026

On Instagram, they went further and claimed the credit:

"As a result of tough questioning from Conservative London Assembly members, Sadiq Khan has been forced to sack Kaya Comer-Scwhartz [sic] as his Deputy Mayor for Policing and Crime. Let's see if her replacement can do a better job…"City Hall Conservatives, Instagram, 19 November 2026 [Instagram]

The video under that caption, titled "KHAN'S DEPUTY SACKED", has Conservative group leader Susan Hall speaking to camera outside City Hall:

"The Deputy Mayor for Policing has been sacked. Because of the difficult questions that the Conservative group have been asking the Deputy Mayor for Policing, Sadiq Khan has been forced to actually shift around the deckchairs on his sinking ship. Kaya, a lovely woman, but absolutely incapable of being the Deputy Mayor for Policing. So let's wait and see how the next one does. We will be asking the questions."Susan Hall AM, City Hall Conservatives video, 19 November 2026 [Facebook] [YouTube]

And to the BBC, Hall said the Conservatives' "running scrutiny of this useless team of deputy mayors has thoroughly exposed their incompetence". [BBC]

Two claims, then. She was sacked. And the Conservatives forced it. Neither stands up.

Fact check 1: She wasn't sacked

Start with the Mayor's own announcement. It does not say she was dismissed. It says: "Today the Mayor is announcing Kaya Comer-Schwartz as London's new Skills Commissioner." It describes the job as leading the new skills and employment powers being devolved to City Hall after the Milburn Review. [Mayor of London] She is still on the Mayor's team. You can call it a demotion. You cannot honestly call it a sacking.

The Conservatives know this, because they said so themselves. Look at their own graphic. "SACKED" in giant red letters across the top. Underneath, in the small print: "Sadiq Khan has demoted Kaya Comer-Schwartz from Deputy Mayor for Police and Crime."

Then listen to Hall's video. In the same breath as "sacked", she says Khan has been forced to "shift around the deckchairs on his sinking ship". Shifting deckchairs is moving people around. That is a reshuffle. Their headline says one thing, their small print and their own spokeswoman say another.

Fact check 2: "Forced" is spin, not fact

Khan gave no reason for moving her. The announcement does not mention her record in the policing role, the Assembly or the Conservatives. [Mayor of London]

Nobody is pretending her time in the job was a success. The BBC noted that critics had called her "ineffective", and that some in City Hall privately agree. But when the BBC's London political editor, Karl Mercer, asked why Khan had acted now, he came up with four possible explanations. Was the Mayor trying to steal the Tories' thunder, or bury a major shake-up, on the very day the Conservatives picked Sir James Cleverly as their 2028 mayoral candidate? Was he strengthening his team for a possible fourth term? Was it an admission that Cleverly will give City Hall a harder time? Or was he building a legacy before deciding that three terms is enough? Not one of the four was "Conservative questioning". [BBC]

The Conservatives know this argument well. When Labour claimed credit for falling Channel crossings, Shadow Home Secretary Chris Philp said the fall was down to wave height in the Channel, not anything the government had done. [Sky News] Something happening after you push for it doesn't mean you caused it. That was the Conservatives' own test for Labour. By that test, their claim about Comer-Schwartz fails too.

And look at the "evidence" they offered. The clip shows no questioning at all, just Susan Hall speaking to camera. It ends by telling viewers to "watch full exchange on YouTube". The YouTube version is the same 28-second clip. No exchange is shown anywhere. [YouTube]

Fact check 3: The Tories were far from the only ones asking

The Conservatives want Londoners to believe that scrutiny of Khan's policing deputy was theirs alone. The London Assembly record says otherwise. Reform UK's Alex Wilson has been putting questions to the Mayor and to Comer-Schwartz on the policing issues Londoners actually live with: shoplifting, police front desks, money spent on climate targets instead of officers, and the vigilantes stepping in where the police are not.

July 2025: shoplifting and her own petition

Wilson put it to the Mayor in writing: "Shoplifting is up 41% in London vs 14% across the rest of the country." [London Assembly] The question was asked on 11 July 2025. The Mayor's answer did not arrive until 23 December, more than five months later.

At the same session, Wilson reminded the Mayor of Comer-Schwartz's own past. In 2017, he noted, she had presented a petition to local residents "against the government's police cuts". His question: "Has she presented a similar level of frustration to you following this year's spending review?" [London Assembly] Police cuts were worth a petition when somebody else was in charge.

August 2025: front desks shut, climate money protected

When the Met proposed closing police station front counters, Wilson questioned Comer-Schwartz directly at an extraordinary meeting of the London Assembly. The closures would save £7 million a year. Meanwhile, the policing budget set aside more than £8 million a year for decarbonising Met buildings. He asked how that was a sensible priority, and pointed out that a Chingford resident would have to travel to Stratford to report a crime. Her answer was that the two sums came from "completely distinct funding pots". Wilson called it "pure word salad" and told her Londoners deserved better. [London Assembly transcript]

August 2026: the performance review that never happened

When she was appointed in October 2024, the Assembly's Police and Crime Committee recommended an annual review of her progress and performance. In August 2026 the committee, on which Wilson sits as Reform UK's lead member, asked her to attend a September meeting for exactly that. The Mayor's Chief of Staff refused. Reviewing her performance, he wrote, was a matter for the Mayor. [London Assembly] Three months later, the Mayor has moved her on without a word of explanation.

September 2026: the vigilantes filling the gap

With the "Pickpocket London" activists tracking, filming and confronting thieves on London's streets, Wilson asked the Mayor: "As Commissioner for Policing and Crime, what is your policing response to the vacuum being filled by these activists?" Six weeks later, the answer came back: report crime to the police, because vigilante approaches "can cause more harm than good". [London Assembly] No word on why Londoners felt the need to do the police's job for them.

19 November 2026: the day of the reshuffle

On the day Khan announced the reshuffle, Wilson's questions tabled for Mayor's Question Time were still on policing. How much of the climate budget at the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime is being paid for with borrowing, and what will the interest cost? How many policing staff work on net zero, and at what cost? And why does the Met solve fewer than 7% of burglaries when Greater Manchester Police manages 14.3%? [London Assembly]

In my view, these are the sharper questions. They are about what Londoners see every day: the shop that keeps getting robbed, the police station that is no longer open, the phone snatched in the street, the burglary nobody solves. They follow the money to where it is going instead of to the front line. And they hold Comer-Schwartz to the standard she once set for others.

"We will be asking the questions"

Susan Hall ends her video with a promise: "We will be asking the questions."

Reform UK already has been. Lib Peck now faces a confirmation hearing at the London Assembly before she can take over London's policing. The questions on shoplifting, front desks, climate spending and unsolved burglaries are still sitting there, unanswered. A change of name on the office door does not answer them.

The Conservatives can call that a sacking if they like. Their own small print says otherwise.

— Steve English
