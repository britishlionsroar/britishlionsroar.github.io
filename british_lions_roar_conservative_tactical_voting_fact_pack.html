When You Know Your Party Will Lose — Beg for Enough Votes to Come Second

THE CONSERVATIVES' NEW STRATEGY: USE DIRTY TACTICS TO COURT LABOUR AND LIB DEM VOTERS — NOT TO WIN, BUT TO HELP BLOCK REFORM UK

When a party can no longer stand convincingly on its own record, eventually it stops asking people to vote for it and starts asking them to vote against somebody else.

In my opinion, that is exactly what we are now watching from the Conservative Party.

Fourteen years in government ended with the worst General Election defeat in Conservative history.

Copying increasing amounts of Reform's political territory hasn't been enough.

Calling themselves “the Right” hasn't been enough.

Another leader hasn't been enough.

So now senior Conservatives are discussing how to attract the votes of people who don't even support the Conservative Party — Labour and Liberal Democrat voters prepared to lend them a tactical vote for one purpose:

STOP REFORM.

So What Actually Happened?

At a More in Common fringe event at Conservative Party Conference, Shadow Scottish Secretary Harriet Cross, Conservative MP Douglas Lumsden, Tory polling expert Lord Hayward and former West Midlands Mayor Andy Street discussed tactical voting against Reform.

Lumsden couldn't have made the calculation much clearer:

“If we are the party in second place, then we are the ones that people will turn to if they want to stop Reform.”

Street said this was already happening, pointing to voters in Solihull who had supported Conservatives to keep Reform out.

He also described the idea of an electoral deal with Reform as “the most stupid thing possible.”

Lord Hayward described Conservative campaigning in Bromley and Bexley which, he said, persuaded Labour and Liberal Democrat voters that they needed to vote Conservative if they wanted to prevent Reform taking control.

Activists were encouraged to establish themselves as one of the top two parties locally — including using the familiar tactical-voting bar charts to make the argument.

Polling presented at the event reportedly found 30% of Labour voters and 37% of Liberal Democrat voters would tactically support a Conservative candidate to stop Reform.

This isn't speculation about what Conservatives might do. They're discussing the strategy themselves.

And the interesting question is why a party which governed Britain for fourteen years now needs people who don't actually want it to govern to help keep it relevant.

The Record They Can't Escape

Before anybody is asked to forget the past because there's another new Conservative leader, it's worth remembering exactly how long the Conservatives had.

May 2010 to July 2024.
Fourteen years.

20,600 Fewer Police Officers

20,600fewer police officers between March 2010 and March 2019.

Police officer numbers in England and Wales fell from around 143,800 to 123,200. That's a 14% reduction. Once population growth is included, the number of police officers per person fell by 19%.

Source: Full Fact — Police officer numbers

And yes, Conservatives will point to the subsequent Police Uplift Programme. They should, because it demonstrates something rather important.

They spent years removing officers and then celebrated putting roughly the same number back.

Restoring headcount does not restore the police-to-population ratio after the population has substantially increased. And it certainly doesn't rebuild everything else that disappeared along the way.

At Least 663 Police Stations Closed

663+police stations closed, according to LBC's FOI investigation.

An LBC Freedom of Information investigation found that at least 663 police stations had closed since 2010.

There isn't a definitive centrally maintained government series for every police-station closure, so that figure should properly be attributed to LBC's investigation rather than presented as a Home Office statistic.

Source: LBC — Police station closures investigation

You can recruit another officer. You can't recruit your way into reopening a police station that's been sold.

You Can't Put an Officer Behind the Front Desk of a Station That You Sold.

Courts Closed — Backlogs Grew

The physical justice system was shrinking too.

Research cited by the Domestic Abuse Commissioner records 164 of 320 magistrates' courts in England and Wales closing between 2010 and 2020 — more than half.

Whatever argument is made for modernisation and consolidation, there is an unavoidable physical reality here.

You Can't Hear Another Criminal Trial in a Courtroom You've Closed.

And by the time the Conservatives left office, the criminal courts were carrying enormous backlogs.

For victims, witnesses and defendants, “backlog” isn't an abstract Whitehall word. It can mean waiting months — sometimes years — for justice.

14 Years: 482 Net Extra Prison Places

482net additional operational prison places between May 2010 and April 2024.

According to the Ministry of Justice, operational prison capacity in England and Wales stood at 89,757 in May 2010. In April 2024 it stood at 90,239.

Net increase after almost fourteen years? 482 places.

Source: Ministry of Justice — prison places built and closed, 2010–2024

Yes, thousands of new places were constructed during those years. But thousands were also lost through closures, dilapidation and other reductions. That's why the net operational-capacity change matters when asking how much additional capacity Britain actually had at the end.

Fourteen years. 482 net additional operational places.

And then politicians wonder why Britain's prisons ran perilously close to full.

7.6 Million NHS Waiting-List Pathways

7.6mreferral-to-treatment pathways waiting at the end of April 2024.

At the end of April 2024 there were 7.6 million referral-to-treatment pathways waiting to start treatment in England.

Because some patients appeared on more than one pathway, NHS England estimated that represented approximately 6.3 million individual people.

Source: NHS England — April 2024 waiting-times statistics

That's the precise statistic. Not 7.6 million individual patients. 7.6 million waits. Around 6.3 million people.

Hardly a distinction that transforms the record into something to boast about.

And Then There's Immigration

This is particularly difficult to reconcile with years of Conservative promises to control immigration.

944,000peak long-term net migration in the year ending March 2023.
1,469,000total long-term immigration during that year.

Both peaks occurred under Conservative government.

Source: Office for National Statistics — revised long-term international migration estimates

Not Under Reform.
Not Under the Current Government.
Under the Conservatives.

That doesn't mean every problem Britain faces today can be attributed to Conservative government. Labour has been responsible for its own decisions since taking office.

But it does mean Conservatives don't get to pretend that July 2024 somehow wiped the previous fourteen years from the record.

“But We Recruited 20,000 Police…”

Yes. After Losing Approximately 20,000 in the First Place.

The uplift matters and should be acknowledged. It doesn't alter what preceded it.

Nor does replacing officers reopen hundreds of police stations. It doesn't reopen magistrates' courts. It doesn't create prison cells. And it doesn't undo the years during which the number of officers per head of population fell.

You can replace an officer.

You can't put them behind the front desk of a police station that you sold.

You can't put defendants before a magistrate in a courthouse that you closed.

And you can't lock another convicted criminal in a prison cell you never built.

“It Would Be Disgraceful…”

There was another remarkable moment at the conference fringe.

Lord Hayward criticised the idea of changing the electoral system specifically to prevent Reform winning, reportedly saying:

“Personally, although I am no fan of Reform, it's disgraceful to say we are going to change the electoral system to stop one political party winning.”

Quite.

Changing the electoral system and tactical voting are obviously different mechanisms.

But there is some considerable irony in condemning an attempt to alter the voting system to stop Reform while participating in a discussion about persuading supporters of other parties to vote tactically with the objective of...

Stopping Reform.

And in my view that looks rather less like a party confidently preparing to return to government than a party fighting to prevent something once almost unimaginable:

Political Oblivion.

Reduced from one of Britain's natural parties of government to another parliamentary group.

Another percentage on the polling chart.

Another party fighting simply to remain relevant.

The people who watched their police station close don't need a tactical-voting bar chart.

The people waiting months or years for justice don't need another Conservative rebrand.

The people who watched immigration reach unprecedented levels after years of promises to control it don't need another speech about how everything has changed.

Copying Reform doesn't erase fourteen years.

Calling themselves “the Right” doesn't erase fourteen years.

Changing leader doesn't erase fourteen years.

Borrowing votes from Labour and Liberal Democrat supporters doesn't erase fourteen years.

Before the Conservatives ask Britain to rescue their party from political oblivion, perhaps there's a rather more important question they should answer:

Why Did So Many of Their Own Voters Want to Leave Them in the First Place?
