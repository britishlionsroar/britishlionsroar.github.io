Rents Rise, Real Wages Barely Move and Tax Thresholds Stay Frozen Until 2031

Britain's average monthly rent has reached £1,411. Meanwhile, workers face an entire decade without an increase in their tax-free personal allowance.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • 27 November 2026

For millions of working households, the financial squeeze comes from three directions.

Higher rents. Weak real wage growth. Frozen tax thresholds.

The latest Office for National Statistics figures put the average UK monthly rent at £1,411, equivalent to £16,932 a year.

Rents have risen by 3.8% over the past twelve months.

Meanwhile, average regular earnings have risen by just 0.2% after inflation.

A decade without an increase in tax-free earnings

In April 2021, the income tax personal allowance was set at £12,570.

It remains £12,570 today.

And it is scheduled to remain £12,570 until April 2031.

An entire decade.

The Conservatives introduced the freeze. Labour subsequently extended it.

As wages rise to compensate for inflation, frozen tax thresholds mean that more of people's earnings become subject to taxation.

Workers can receive a pay rise without experiencing a meaningful improvement in their standard of living.

The rental squeeze is far from over

Property specialists Hamptons forecast that rents on newly agreed tenancies will rise by a further 14.2% between 2026 and 2029.

That is almost twice the forecast increase in house prices.

For households already struggling to save a deposit, rising rents can make escaping the rental market even more difficult.

Meanwhile, from April 2027, property income tax rates for landlords will increase by four percentage points.

The Office for Budget Responsibility has warned that successive measures reducing landlords' returns risk restricting rental supply and increasing rents.

What does a pay rise actually buy?

The real test of improving living standards is not simply whether somebody's salary increases.

It is whether they have more money left after paying their taxes, rent, energy bills, food and other essential costs.

With rental costs rising, real wage growth barely moving and tax thresholds frozen for years to come, that remains a considerable challenge.
