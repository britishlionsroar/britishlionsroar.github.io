Chapter 03

Design Philosophy

Electrasthetics is built around one central idea: every visible component should feel intentional, coordinated and appropriate to the interior around it.

Form with purpose. Premium appearance, practical installation and long-term adaptability must work together.
On this page Design intent Core principles Installation logic Materials and finishes Range identity
01
Design intent

The housing should improve the whole interior—not merely contain a component.

The visible enclosure is the part the customer judges. It therefore needs the same attention given to cabinetry, lighting and furniture hardware.

Electrasthetics products should look designed rather than improvised. Their proportions, edge treatment, fixing method and finish should remain consistent even when the electrical modules behind them differ.

The goal is quiet confidence: distinctive enough to feel premium, restrained enough to suit many interiors.

Core principles

Six rules for every product in the range.

01

Conceal the fixings

Visible screw heads should be avoided wherever a secure snap, hook, rail or hidden fastening can do the job better.

02

Keep the front clean

The face should be visually simple, with controlled gaps, consistent radii and no unnecessary moulding detail.

03

Design for replacement

Electrical modules should remain serviceable and replaceable without destroying finished cabinetry or the outer housing.

04

Share the architecture

Backplates, insert sizes and closure methods should be reused wherever possible to reduce development and increase compatibility.

05

Respect compact spaces

Depth, cable bend radius and access must suit campervan walls, furniture voids and surface-mount installations.

06

Offer visual choice

Colour and finish options should allow the range to coordinate with timber, painted cabinetry, metalwork and upholstery.

02
Installation logic

Simple from the front. Thoughtful behind it.

Premium appearance is only useful when the product remains practical to fit, wire, remove and service.

The preferred architecture uses a separate backplate and outer housing. The backplate can carry the electrical module and provide screw, adhesive or mixed mounting options. The outer housing then attaches using a bottom hook rail and concealed retention points.

A

Backplate first

Installers can secure and wire the functional part before fitting the finished outer cover.

B

Multiple mounting options

Screws, 3M VHB adhesive or a combined method can suit different substrates and access conditions.

C

Controlled removal

The cover should release deliberately for maintenance without appearing loose during normal use.

The finished product should hide complexity from the customer without making life harder for the installer.

03
Materials and finishes

The range should feel at home in both modern and character interiors.

A single black plastic finish would repeat the problem Electrasthetics is intended to solve.

The initial range can be developed in print-friendly engineering polymers, with finish options chosen around durability, repeatability and visual fit. The housing geometry should also remain suitable for later production methods if volume justifies them.

Core neutral finishes

Warm white, soft black, graphite, light grey and cabinetry-matched colours provide broad compatibility for professional converters.

Character finishes

Hammered copper, aged brass, textured stone and specialist paint systems can create higher-margin variants and limited collections.

Finishes should be offered as a controlled palette, not an uncontrolled list of colours.

04
Range identity

Different products should remain visibly related.

Consistency will be created through shared proportions, edge radii, shadow gaps, finish options and closure details.

A downlight housing, router enclosure and switch backbox do not need to look identical. They do need to look as though the same design team created them for the same vehicle.

01

Recognisable silhouette

Soft geometric forms and controlled depth create a family resemblance across different product sizes.

02

Consistent detailing

Shared edge treatment, reveal lines and concealed fixing produce continuity at close range.

03

Brand restraint

The product should remain the hero; branding should be subtle, durable and never visually intrusive.

The design philosophy creates a clear filter for future development: if a product cannot improve appearance, installation and serviceability together, it does not belong in the range.
