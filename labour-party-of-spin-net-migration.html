Labour: The Party of Spin

77,000? Britain took in 629,000 foreigners last year and lost 137,000 of its own. Here's what Labour's headline is hiding.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • 26 November 2026
  • 738,000 people moved to Britain in the year to June 2026. 629,000 of them were foreign nationals.
  • 245,000 Britons left. Only 109,000 came home.
  • Strip out Britons and Europeans, and net migration from the rest of the world was +258,000. That's more than three times Labour's headline.
  • 73,000 arrivals came to claim asylum, almost the whole headline figure on their own.
  • 241,000 settlement grants, the most since 2010. A record 320,000 citizenship applications.
  • Of the 135,000 non-EU “work” arrivals, 75,000 were dependants. Only 60,000 were workers.
  • Asylum appeals waiting in the tribunal: 6,300 in 2022. 90,000 now.
PURPOSE: To show, from the ONS's and the Home Office's own figures published today, what Labour's 77,000 headline leaves out, and what Reform UK would do instead.

“This Government has restored control.”

That was Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood this morning. “We have brought net migration to 77,000, down 92% from its peak under the previous Government.”

77,000. The lowest since 1997, if you leave out Covid. Ministers want you to read it, nod, and move on.

Don't.

77,000 is a subtraction, not a headcount

Net migration is arrivals minus departures. It is not the number of people who came.

The number who came was 738,000. In twelve months, 550,000 people arrived from outside Europe and 79,000 from the EU. The other 109,000 were Britons coming home.

That's 629,000 foreign nationals in a single year. Labour has turned that into “77,000” and called it control.

The British exodus

Here's how the trick works. 245,000 British nationals left the country. Only 109,000 came back. Britain lost 137,000 of its own people in a year.

That's what makes Labour's number small. Fewer foreigners didn't arrive. Britons left.

Look at the rest of the world on its own: +258,000. That's more than three times the figure Labour is boasting about.

Labour isn't controlling immigration. It's losing its own taxpayers and calling the gap a success.

Asylum: almost the whole headline on its own

Of the arrivals from outside Europe, 73,000 came to claim asylum. That's almost the entire headline figure from one route.

And the bill hasn't gone away. 93,000 people are still living on Home Office asylum support.

Fewer coming. More staying. For good.

This is the part Labour really doesn't want you to see.

In the year to September, the Home Office handed out 241,000 settlement grants. That's up 53% in a year and the highest since 2010. Counting EU settled status, 545,000 people were given indefinite leave to stay.

Citizenship applications hit a record 320,000. 265,000 people were made British citizens. The largest groups were Indian nationals (27,700) and Pakistani nationals (19,400).

Settlement is permanent. Citizenship is for ever. These are the Boriswave arrivals being locked in for life, on Labour's watch.

Labour counts who's arriving. It doesn't want you counting who's staying.

The “workers” who aren't workers

Immigration is always sold to us as workers filling jobs. Here's what the “work” route actually brought in.

135,000 arrivals from outside Europe came for work. Only 60,000 were the workers. The other 75,000 were dependants.

Five dependants for every four workers. They all need homes, school places, GP appointments and hospital beds.

Refused, but still here

Labour will brag that the queue for first asylum decisions is down to 41,000. That queue hasn't gone. It's moved to court.

Asylum and protection appeals waiting in the immigration tribunal: 6,300 in June 2022. 90,000 in June 2026. That's fourteen times higher.

Enforced returns in the last year: 9,500. Up 1%. That's 9,500 in a country where 90,000 asylum appeals are queuing in the courts.

Labour: the party of spin

Put it all together. 629,000 foreigners arrived. 137,000 Britons were lost. Settlement hit a 16-year high and citizenship applications a record. Most “work” migrants were dependants. 90,000 asylum appeals are clogging the courts. Enforced removals are flat.

And Labour calls that “restored control”.

Even the ONS can't say how big the real number is. Its own likely range for net migration runs from 31,000 to 128,000. The figure Labour is celebrating is a provisional estimate, and the British part of it is a projection.

Labour hasn't fixed immigration. It's found a smaller number to put on the press release.
