Britain's Future Is Islamic

The world's leading religious forecaster has run the numbers. Christian Britain halves. The Muslim population nearly triples. The rest of the country shrinks. Only one thing changes the trajectory, and it's on the ballot paper.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • November 2026
  • Pew Research Center projects Britain's Muslim population will rise from 4.3 million to 11.5 million by 2070, an increase of 167%.
  • Over the same period, the number of Christians in Britain falls by 15 million, from 33.3 million to 18.2 million.
  • Muslims in Britain increase by 7.2 million while non-Muslims fall by 2.7 million. All of Britain's population growth to 2070 comes from the Muslim population.
  • In 2020, one in ten religious Britons was Muslim. By 2070, it is one in three.
  • Worldwide, Muslims overtake Christians as the largest religious group by 2035.
  • Pew's projections assume recent migration carries on. Reform UK is the only party with a realistic chance of government that would stop it.
PURPOSE: To set out, using Pew Research Center's own figures, what its November 2026 religious projections mean for Britain, and to ask which party has a plan to change the course they describe.

On 18 November 2026, Pew Research Center published The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2020–2070. It is the most detailed forecast of its kind ever produced: seven religious groups, 201 countries and territories, fifty years. Pew built it with demographers at the Center for Advanced Studies of Population and Religion at Krakow University of Economics. This is no campaign pamphlet. It is the reference work that governments, universities and newsrooms will be quoting for the next decade.

The headline finding is global. By 2035, less than a decade from now, Muslims will outnumber Christians worldwide: 2.57 billion against 2.52 billion. By 2070 there will be more than 3.6 billion Muslims, 36% of humanity, against just under 3 billion Christians. Over the fifty years, the number of Muslims grows by 80%. Everyone else, taken together, grows by 11%.

Pew explains why in one phrase: Muslim growth is fuelled by “dual demographic advantages of relative youth and high fertility.” Between 2020 and 2025, Muslim women worldwide averaged 3.1 children, against 2.0 for everyone else. In 2020 the median Muslim was 24 years old; the median non-Muslim was 33.

But the global picture isn't the story. Britain is.

What Pew says about Britain

Pew's country tables for the United Kingdom are blunt.

In 2020, Britain had 33.3 million Christians, 49.4% of the population. By 2070, Pew projects 18.2 million, or 25.4%. Fifteen million gone. A Christian population cut by 45% within a single lifetime.

In 2020, Britain had 4.3 million Muslims, 6.4% of the population. By 2070, Pew projects 11.5 million, or 16.0%. That is a rise of 167%. The Muslim population grows in every decade of the projection, adding between 1.2 and 1.6 million people each time.

Now look at the other side of the ledger. Over the same fifty years, the number of Muslims in Britain rises by 7.2 million. The number of non-Muslims (Christians, people of no religion and every other faith combined) falls by 2.7 million, from 63.1 million to 60.4 million. Put the two together and Britain's overall population grows by just 4.5 million, from 67.4 million to 71.9 million. In other words, every bit of Britain's population growth comes from the Muslim population, and the non-Muslim population goes backwards.

In 2020, there were nearly eight Christians in Britain for every Muslim. By 2070, there are 1.6. Among Britons who follow any religion at all, one in ten was Muslim in 2020. By 2070, it is one in three.

Pew's tables stop at 2070. The trend doesn't. In the final decade of the projection, the gap between Christians and Muslims closes by more than five percentage points. Carry that line forward and Islam overtakes Christianity as Britain's largest religion before the end of the 2080s. That extrapolation is ours, not Pew's, but it is Pew's line, drawn on.

That is what “Britain's future is Islamic” means. Not a slogan. A trajectory.

Why it is happening

Two engines drive it: birth rates and migration.

On birth rates, Pew's earlier study of Europe, published in 2017, found that Muslim women in the UK averaged 2.9 children, about one more than non-Muslim women. A gap that size, compounding generation after generation, produces exactly what the 2026 tables show.

On migration, the same 2017 study found that around 43% of all migrants who came to the UK between mid-2010 and mid-2016 were Muslim, roughly 690,000 people in six years.

The 2026 report shows how much migration matters. Across Europe, Pew projects the Muslim share of the population more than doubling, from 6% to 13% by 2070, with a net gain of 27 million Muslims through migration. With no migration at all, the Muslim share would reach only 9%.

This is the point the political class never wants to discuss. Pew's projections assume recent trends carry on, and that includes migration. No government can easily change a birth rate. Migration is a policy choice. Every figure in the table above rests on the assumption that Westminster keeps making the choice it has made for a quarter of a century. Reform UK's own research puts net immigration between January 2021 and June 2024 alone at 2.6 million, the so-called Boriswave.

Which means the trajectory can be changed, by changing the government.

The only party with a plan

In the current political climate, only one party has both a realistic prospect of winning the next general election and the policies to change this trajectory: Reform UK.

Labour and the Conservatives built the migration numbers that feed these projections, and they did it in the teeth of their own promises. In 1974 the Conservatives pledged to cut new immigration to “a small and inescapable minimum”. In 2010 David Cameron promised to bring net migration down to the tens of thousands a year, “no ifs, no buts”. By 2015 it was more than three times that, and the same pledge went back into the 2015 and 2017 manifestos regardless. In 2019 Boris Johnson's manifesto promised that “overall numbers will come down”. Instead, net migration hit a record 906,000 in the year to June 2023. In 2024 Labour's manifesto promised it “will reduce net migration”, while the boats kept coming and the Boriswave began reaching permanent settlement on Labour's watch.

Promise after promise, manifesto after manifesto, for half a century. The voters asked for less every time, and every time they got more. Reform has published, in detail, how it would reverse it.

Stop the boats: Operation Fortress

On day one, the Prime Minister convenes COBRA and directs the armed forces to deploy against small boats under a naval-led Joint Task Force. Boats are intercepted and migrants are returned to French or Belgian shores. No asylum claims are considered.

Deport those with no right to be here: Operation Restoring Justice

Leave the European Convention on Human Rights, repeal the Human Rights Act, and pass an Illegal Migration (Mass Deportation) Bill placing a legal duty on the Home Secretary to remove anyone without leave to remain. A new UK Deportation Command, up to 24,000 detention places within 18 months, and up to five charter flights a day.

End the asylum scam

Suspend the asylum system for a full parliamentary term. Close resettlement routes. End hotel and dispersal accommodation. Re-examine every refugee status granted in the past five years, more than 400,000 people in all.

Deport foreign criminals automatically on conviction. On 30 June 2026 there were 10,134 foreign nationals in prisons in England and Wales, about one prisoner in every eight. And that counts only those behind bars. Every foreign national convicted and handed a community sentence, a suspended sentence or a fine is on top of that figure.

Reverse the Boriswave: net negative immigration

Abolish indefinite leave to remain, including existing grants, and replace it with a five-year renewable visa with higher salary, English language and good-character tests, and no access to welfare. Raise the qualifying period for British citizenship to seven years. Restrict welfare and social housing to British citizens.

The numbers involved are huge. Oxford University's Migration Observatory estimates that between 620,000 and 820,000 non-EU migrants already held indefinite leave to remain at the end of 2024. On top of that, the Centre for Policy Studies found that around 2 million people arrived on visas between January 2021 and June 2024 that could lead to settlement, and that between 742,000 and 1,224,000 of them are likely to be granted it. Under Reform's plan, those who already hold it would have to reapply under the tougher rules, and the Boriswave arrivals would never get permanent settlement at all. Reform's head of policy has said hundreds of thousands would ultimately lose their settled status.

Taken together: an end to mass immigration, an end to small boat crossings, and a start on reducing the number of people already here who have no right to remain. That is the one lever that moves Pew's numbers.

Pew has told Britain where it is heading. A child born in Britain this year will be 44 in 2070, and will grow up in the country those tables describe unless the next government changes course. The question at the next general election is not abstract. It is whether Britain chooses its own future, or lets a projection choose it.
