★ Where This Started, Where It Can Go ★

This Begins With Campervans. It Does Not End There.

Everything in the main proposal was developed from real problems encountered by campervan and motorhome owners. That starting market remains essential — and the same underlying problem reaches much further.

Where This Came From
Real problems, solved for real owners first

The main proposal was developed from problems encountered directly by campervan and motorhome owners:

  • Electrical components designed by unrelated manufacturers
  • Incompatible shapes and mounting systems
  • Visible screws and improvised backboxes
  • Finished walls that are difficult or risky to cut
  • Thin panels with little or no usable cavity behind them
  • Unattractive surface wiring and trunking
  • No coordinated family of housings, controls and cable management

That starting market remains essential. Individual owners, self-builders, retrofit customers and people upgrading existing campervans and motorhomes are not a secondary audience. They are the first direct retail market, the people already searching for solutions, and the customers who establish what a properly designed finished product can retail for.

Electrasthetics is not a collection of individual products. It is a common design language and mounting standard capable of supporting an expanding ecosystem of compatible electrical accessories.

What This Actually Is
Not a socket surround. Not a switch housing. A universal surface-mounted electrical architecture.

The electrical function is almost secondary. What's actually been developed is a universal surface-mounted architecture — a way of fixing, closing, finishing and coordinating electrical components against thin walls or no cavity at all. It happens to launch into the campervan market first, because that's where the problem was identified and where Anvil already has relationships. The same architecture works wherever the same physical constraint exists.

Three Parallel Markets
Direct retail, professional converters, and manufacturers

These three support one another. Direct retail demonstrates demand and achievable selling price. Professional converters create repeat orders. Manufacturers create volume — and Anvil already knows this market from the inside, because Anvil is one.

Direct Retail

Individual owners, self-builders, retrofit customers and enthusiasts buying one housing, one panel or a coordinated collection for their own vehicle or unit.

Professional Converters

Businesses purchasing repeatable components in quantity across multiple builds — hundreds of UK companies alone.

Manufacturers

Motorhome, caravan, welfare unit, horsebox, mobile office and modular accommodation manufacturers building hundreds or thousands of units a year — potentially much larger customers than individual converters.

Why doesn't this already exist? Because every manufacturer builds their own ecosystem. Nobody has attempted to create a manufacturer-independent mounting standard.

Why Now
The gap nobody else has filled

For decades, existing electrical component manufacturers have concentrated on producing individual switches, sockets, controllers and mounting hardware, each within their own closed product ecosystem. Every one of them is very good at what they build. None has attempted to create a manufacturer-independent mounting standard capable of accepting components from multiple sources whilst maintaining one consistent, coordinated appearance across an entire installation. That's not an oversight — it's simply not the business any of them are in. Electrasthetics sits above that layer, filling a gap none of them were positioned to fill, and that gap is the actual competitive position, not any single product in the range.

The biggest gap in earlier drafts: where are the manufacturers?

Manufacturers
Anvil already knows this customer — because Anvil is one

Professional converters matter, but manufacturers building hundreds or thousands of units a year are potentially much bigger customers than individual installers. This isn't a separate market from converters — it's the same problem at industrial scale:

Manufacturers are continually balancing build time, labour cost, warranty claims and component sourcing. Electrasthetics offers them a different pitch entirely from the retail or converter argument:

  • Reduce installation time on the production line
  • Standardise electrical layouts across model ranges
  • Reduce panel damage risk during manufacture
  • Simplify staff training — one system, not a dozen vendor parts
  • Reduce stock complexity and supplier count
  • Create premium trim packages sellable as an upgrade tier
  • Create manufacturer-exclusive colours or finishes as a differentiator

Two Different Sales Arguments
Time sells it to the builder. Appearance sells it to the customer.

The aesthetic improvement remains central, but it is not the only commercial argument.

What Sells It to a Professional Builder

  • Faster installation
  • Repeatable components
  • Reduced skilled-labour time
  • Fewer precision recesses cut into finished panels
  • Less risk of scrapping an expensive panel after a bad cut
  • Easier servicing and replacement
  • A premium option sellable to the end customer
  • One installation method standardised across several products

What Sells It to an Individual Customer

  • A coordinated interior
  • Attractive, concealed fixings
  • Easier retrofit
  • Less damage to finished walls
  • The same visual language extended across the vehicle
  • A bespoke-looking result without bespoke fabrication

The objective is not to sell electrical accessories. The objective is to become the standard they all fit into.

Each new product added to the Electrasthetics range increases the value of every existing product already in the ecosystem. That's exactly how ecosystems become difficult to compete with — it's the same principle behind Apple, Milwaukee, Makita and LEGO. None of them win because any single product is unbeatable. They win because adding another product strengthens everything already bought.

Design standard. Common mounting language. Compatible product ecosystem. Multiple manufacturers. Shared architecture. Premium finishes. Expansion into dozens of industries. Taken together, that's no longer a switch surround — it's a platform, and a genuine long-term product roadmap for whoever owns it.

The main proposal begins with campervans and motorhomes because that is the strongest, most thoroughly researched and most immediately accessible starting point. The immediate opportunity is direct retail and UK professional converters. The larger opportunity is repeat B2B supply to manufacturers across the UK and Europe. The long-term opportunity is for Electrasthetics to become the design and mounting standard used across multiple forms of mobile and compact accommodation.
