The Purged and the Exiled vs The Principled Walkaways

Why the "Reform is just reheated Tory" line collapses the moment you ask who actually locked the door.

  • Your Party has two MPs — Jeremy Corbyn and Zarah Sultana — both former Labour MPs. Restore Britain has one MP — Rupert Lowe — a former Reform UK MP.
  • Corbyn, Sultana and Lowe were all suspended, blocked from standing, or told there was "no way back" by their former party leaderships before they left.
  • The former Conservative MPs now sitting for Reform UK — Lee Anderson, Robert Jenrick, Danny Kruger, Andrew Rosindell and Suella Braverman — made conscious, mid-term decisions to walk out while they still had the choice to stay.
  • Jenrick was sacked only after Kemi Badenoch's team obtained evidence he was already secretly preparing to defect.
  • The distinction is not spin. It is the difference between being pushed and choosing to jump.

"But Reform is just a reheated Tory party full of ex-Tories!"

It's one of the most common lines thrown at Reform UK by its critics. So let's actually look at it.

Your Party consists of just two MPs: Jeremy Corbyn and Zarah Sultana — both former Labour MPs. Restore Britain consists of just one MP: Rupert Lowe — a former Reform UK MP. Yet the same people who insist Reform is "reheated Tory" don't call Your Party "reheated Labour," or Restore Britain "Reform with a different badge."

There is a much more important difference than party colour. In any other workplace, if you quit because your boss made your position impossible, or because you found somewhere better, you walked away. If your boss permanently bars you from the building, replaces you, and makes clear you will never be allowed back, you were fired. And if your boss discovers you've already written your resignation letter and shouts "you're fired!" five minutes before you hand it over, that's procedural theatre — they didn't make you leave, they merely discovered you were already leaving.

The Purged and Exiled

Jeremy Corbyn

Corbyn had the Labour whip removed in 2020 after the Equality and Human Rights Commission found the party had breached the Equality Act, and he was accused of failing to fully accept its findings on antisemitism. The suspension was never fully lifted at the parliamentary level, and despite "unanimous support" from his local constituency party, Corbyn was blocked from standing as a Labour candidate at the 2024 general election. Once he confirmed he would stand as an independent, he was formally expelled from the party. Labour didn't wait for him to walk; it locked the door first, then framed his departure as his own choice.

Zarah Sultana

Sultana technically resigned from Labour — but look at why. She was one of seven Labour MPs who had the whip withdrawn in July 2024 for voting for an SNP amendment to scrap the two-child benefit cap. When the initial six-month suspension period ended, four of the seven had the whip restored and rejoined the parliamentary party — but Sultana remained suspended after continuing to criticise government policy. She left Labour in July 2025 to co-found a new party with Corbyn. She wasn't handed a way back in. She was pushed out of the window and it got called a jump.

Rupert Lowe

Lowe didn't get a parliamentary timeout either. Reform UK suspended his whip in March 2025 following complaints of bullying, and he sat as an independent for a year before founding his own party, Restore Britain. Crucially, Nigel Farage told The Telegraph that Lowe "knows there's no way back," accusing him of wanting to "cause maximum destruction and damage" to the party. That is not a leadership leaving the door ajar. That is the leadership publicly bolting it shut.

Compare all of that with the former Conservatives who now sit for Reform.

The Principled Walkaways

Lee Anderson

Yes, Anderson had the Conservative whip suspended — stripped in February 2024 after he declined to apologise for saying "Islamists" had "got control" of Sadiq Khan and Keir Starmer. But that was a parliamentary suspension, not expulsion from the party. He then chose to switch to Reform UK himself, becoming its first MP before the general election. The Conservatives didn't blacklist him from the party. He walked, because he wanted to.

Robert Jenrick

Jenrick was technically fired — but only because Kemi Badenoch's team obtained what she called "irrefutable evidence that he was plotting in secret to defect" in a manner designed to be as damaging as possible to the party. A Conservative source said Jenrick had been having dinner with Farage and speaking to people within Westminster about a possible move, and that his team had already spoken to journalists about defecting. Within minutes of being sacked, Jenrick appeared alongside Farage at a Reform press conference to confirm the defection. That's the boss finding the resignation letter on the printer and shouting "you're fired!" before it can be handed over. He'd already chosen to leave — the Conservatives just found out first.

Danny Kruger

Kruger defected from the Conservatives to Reform UK in September 2025, writing an op-ed in The Times headlined "The Tories are done, the voters aren't coming back." He said plainly: "I'm leaving because I think the party is over." Nobody expelled him. He looked at the party and concluded it had nothing left to offer.

Andrew Rosindell

Rosindell, the Tory shadow minister for foreign affairs, resigned his post and left the Conservative Party — a party he had belonged to since he was 14 — saying "the time has come to put country before party." A Conservative source claimed he had "threatened to defect for months," but the decision to actually go was his. Nobody barred the door. He opened it himself.

Suella Braverman

Braverman resigned from the Conservative Party after 30 years of membership, telling a Reform UK event "I feel like I've come home," and calling Nigel Farage the only politician who had been "courageously consistent." Farage said the party had been in talks with her for around a year before the move. That is a deliberate, considered walk — not an eviction.

So Let's Summarise

The Purged and Exiled — Your Party and Restore Britain: Corbyn, Sultana and Lowe were suspended, blocked from standing, or told there was no way back by their own party leaderships. Their political homes locked the doors before they ever tried to walk through them.

The Principled Walkaways — Reform UK: Every former Conservative MP who crossed to Reform made a conscious, willing decision to go before being permanently expelled or told there was no way back. They weren't unemployed political rejects hunting for a new home. They were sitting MPs who concluded the Conservative Party had abandoned its principles and chose to leave while they still had another choice.

That is the difference. Reform UK isn't a reheated Tory party. It's where Conservatives go when they finally admit the Conservative Party is no longer conservative.
