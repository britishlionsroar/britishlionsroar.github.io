Labour Never Accepted Your Vote. Now Burnham Wants It Overturned.

The Prime Minister's own ally says he is “determined” to take Britain back into the EU. The polls he is betting on fall apart the moment voters are told the price.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • November 2026
  • Burnham signed the first stage of his “Brexit reset” in Brussels on Friday. The EU is already demanding British taxpayers subsidise European students' tuition fees.

  • 71% of 2016 Leave voters say chasing EU membership is the wrong priority. Among 2024 Reform UK voters it is 79%.
  • More than 500,000 EU nationals have become British citizens since the referendum was announced: around 40% of the Leave winning margin.
PURPOSE: To set out, from the record, what Labour is now openly planning on Europe, what voters are not being told, and whose say is being diluted.

The next general election in Britain has just been turned into something else entirely: a referendum on whether the country goes back into the European Union.

That is the message from the last 72 hours in British politics. Andy Burnham and the Labour government have made their plan clear. They want to push Britain back into the EU and overturn the Brexit vote.

Just look at what we have seen.

On Friday in Brussels, Burnham signed off the first stage of his so-called “Brexit reset”: a youth mobility scheme, an agreement on food and animal checks, and a link between British and EU carbon trading. Before the ink was dry, Brussels added its price. The EU says none of it will be put into effect until Britain charges European students the same tuition fees as British students. Put simply, the British taxpayer foots the bill. Power, control and money handed to Brussels, for nothing obvious in return.

Labour lines up behind Rejoin

At the same time, Labour figures were queuing up to tell the government to go further, ignore the democratic vote of 2016 and rejoin the EU in full.

David Lammy, Starmer's former Deputy Prime Minister, wrote in The Times on Tuesday: “Burnham's Labour should go into the next election clear that its intention is to rejoin the EU.” Peter Kyle, Labour's former business secretary, backed a manifesto pledge to negotiate membership and dismissed any halfway house. Ed Miliband, now Foreign Secretary, told the BBC on Sunday that “all options” are on the table. Rachel Reeves, sacked as chancellor by Burnham when he entered Downing Street, wrote in the Sunday Times last month that Brexit had made Britain “poorer, weaker and less secure”, and that “Britain's future lies in the European Union.” This is the same Reeves who helped write Labour's 2024 manifesto, which ruled out rejoining.

It is all so transparent. Everywhere you look, Labour politicians are calling on a Labour government to overturn one of the largest democratic votes in British history. On the best estimates, it is a vote that nearly two-thirds of Labour's own seats backed: 148 Labour constituencies voted Leave, against 84 that voted Remain.

The Prime Minister's real position

And so is the Prime Minister. A senior ally told The Times this weekend that Burnham “wants to rejoin. That's the truth of it.” The same source said Burnham believes it deeply and thinks the public is on his side. None of it is new. At his party conference in September, Burnham said rejoining “could go all the way”.

The gamble

The political gamble is clear enough. Burnham and Labour are pursuing a very deliberate strategy. He has apologised for Labour's handling of Gaza, saying the party “didn't get it right” and was “too slow to call for a ceasefire”. That is a pitch aimed squarely at the Labour voters who defected to the Greens, two-thirds of whom cited Gaza, according to polling reported by The Guardian. He has promised to fight the next election on scrapping first past the post for proportional representation. He has called for an “honest discussion” about bringing back free movement. He has just signed a youth mobility scheme. And now he wants to rejoin the EU, all to lock down the liberal-left vote.

He hopes to assemble a coalition of Remain voters, Green voters, Liberal Democrats and 2024 Labour voters alienated by Keir Starmer. At the same time he is trying to peel voters away from the so-called centre (the Conservatives, masquerading as centre-right), from Reform UK (the actual right), from Restore Britain (the fake “extreme right” the Conservatives built) and from the Islamic bloc (the Islamic-Greens).

Along the way, Burnham and his allies reassure themselves with the opinion polls. They tell one another that most people now want to rejoin. Some of them do not even hide why they think the numbers have moved. Writing in the New Statesman, Caroline Lucas put Remain's rising support partly down to demographics, noting that “more than six million people have died in the UK since the referendum”. In other words, the people old enough and wise enough to fight this tooth and nail are, as they see it, mostly dead or in care homes. So they think they will get away with it.

But the trouble with gamblers, as anyone who has spent time in a casino will tell you, is that they spend so long telling you how they are going to win that they never stop to think about how they might lose. And when it comes to Burnham's gamble on Brexit, it is not hard to see how it could backfire badly at the next election.

How it backfires

The most obvious way is that by reopening the Brexit wars, Burnham hands Nigel Farage and Reform UK a huge opportunity to build momentum. Betrayed by the Tories on immigration for 14 years, whatever the charismatic speakers and endless promises said. Betrayed on Brexit, first by the Tories and now by Labour. And betrayed by a Westminster class, the Uniparty, that has no interest in treating the British people's values, voice and votes with respect.

And despite what Burnham, Lammy and Labour claim, the polls are nowhere near as kind to Rejoin as they would like you to believe.

The polls Labour won't quote

YouGov surveyed more than 12,000 people in October and November and tested what happens when voters are told the actual terms. Offer the old deal back, with the rebate and every opt-out intact, and Rejoin wins easily. Make Britain join the euro and lose the rebate, which is exactly what the EU is expected to demand, and support for rejoining falls to 35–36%, with 43% voting to stay out. Opinium found the same thing last week: just 36% say Britain should rejoin, while 55% want to stay outside the EU in some form.

Even before the price is mentioned, more people think chasing EU membership is the wrong priority for the country (43%) than the right one (38%). Among 2016 Leave voters it is not close: 71% say it is the wrong priority and just 16% the right one. Among 2024 Reform UK voters the figure is 79%, and among 2024 Conservative voters 70%.

Who gets to vote this time?

Then there is the question Labour would rather you did not ask.

Burnham has already accepted that any deal must go back to the public. The 2016 result was Leave 17,410,742, Remain 16,141,241: a margin of just 1,269,501 votes. Since the referendum was announced, more than 500,000 EU nationals have become British citizens, with full voting rights. That is around 40% of the Leave margin before a single vote is cast. Behind them are 5.8 million people granted status under the EU Settlement Scheme, most of them on the road to permanent settlement and, in time, citizenship.

Labour has form here. Its 2019 manifesto promised “full voting rights to all UK residents”. And the idea has not gone away. In September, Lib Dem MP Manuela Perteghella tabled an amendment to Labour's Representation of the People Bill to hand general election votes to qualifying EU citizens and other resident foreign nationals. Among its co-sponsors was John McDonnell, a sitting Labour MP and Labour's former shadow chancellor. It was not put to a vote this time. But it is on the table, in the same Bill that lowers the voting age to 16, backed by Burnham's future coalition partners and by Labour's own benches.

Ten years ago, 17.4 million people were told their vote would be respected. Labour has spent most of the decade since working out how to make it not count. The voters Burnham is betting on forgetting are the very voters who will decide whether he wins. And there is only one party that has never wavered on that vote, never tried to rerun it, and never treated the British people as an obstacle to be waited out. It is Reform UK.

Burnham thinks the house always wins. He may find that this time, the house is the British voter.
