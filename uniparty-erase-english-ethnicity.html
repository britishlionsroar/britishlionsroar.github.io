The Uniparty's Plan to Erase English Ethnicity – and Not One MP Stood Up to Stop It

A Lib Dem bill backed by Labour and the Tories. A Prime Minister who hadn't read it. And two days later, a civil service redesign of the census that delivers it without a vote.

By Steve English, Independent British writer and editor · November 2026

  • A bill to add "Black English", "Asian English" and "Mixed English" to the census was waved through the Commons on 10 November. Not one Labour, Conservative or Lib Dem MP objected, and nine put their names to it.
  • The next day, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he wanted to support it, while admitting on the record that he had not looked at the detail.
  • Two days after the bill, civil servants published a redesigned census question that lets anyone of any background tick "English". No bill needed. No vote needed.
  • The state has never counted the White English. The new form will make sure it never can.
PURPOSE: To set out, in their own words and using their own figures, what Parliament and Whitehall are doing to English ethnicity in the census, and why an inherited ethnic identity and an adopted national identity must stay separate in official statistics.

"Madam Deputy Speaker, do you have to be white to be English?"

That is how Liberal Democrat MP Josh Babarinde opened his speech in the House of Commons on Tuesday 10 November 2026, introducing the English Ethnic Identity (Census Particulars) Bill, which he calls the "St George's Bill". Its purpose is to add "Black English", "Asian English" and "Mixed English" to the ethnicity question on the census in England. [Hansard]

Under Commons rules, any MP could have stood up and spoken against it. None did. The official record reads simply: "Question put and agreed to." There was no objection and no vote.

Then look at who presented it: Josh Babarinde, David Lammy, Sir James Cleverly, Adam Jogee, Ben Obese-Jecty, Victoria Collins, Munira Wilson, Layla Moran and Mark Pritchard. Labour, Conservative and Liberal Democrat, side by side. [Hansard]

Not one Labour, Conservative or Lib Dem MP objected, and nine of them put their names to it. If you ever wanted a picture of the Uniparty, there it is, on a single order paper.

The Prime Minister who hadn't read it

The following day, at Prime Minister's Questions, Lib Dem leader Sir Ed Davey teed it up. He told the House that the idea that people born and raised in England cannot be English because they are not white was a "racist ideology", and praised the Conservative leader who had rejected it, "to her great credit". Then he asked the Prime Minister to back the Bill. [Hansard]

Andy Burnham's answer is worth reading twice:

"I have not looked at the detail of the Bill, but I stand here thinking that I would want to support it, to directly take on the poisonous politics that we have seen from some in this House who are seeking to divide us."Prime Minister Andy Burnham, PMQs, 11 November 2026

The Prime Minister of this country backed changing how an ethnic group is recorded on the national census, and told Parliament in the same breath that he hadn't read the Bill. That tells you this was never about the detail. It was about the signal.

The Bill is the sideshow

A Ten Minute Rule bill is a backbench device. It rarely becomes law without the government giving it time. This one has been set down for a second reading on Friday 23 April 2027, St George's Day, naturally. [Parliament]

So if the Bill is unlikely to pass, why does it matter? Because the change it asks for is already being built somewhere else, by people nobody elected.

What Whitehall published two days later

On Thursday 12 November, two days after the Bill, the Government Statistical Service, which is part of the Office for National Statistics, published its Autumn 2026 update on a new ethnicity standard. [GSS] This is not an academic paper. The ONS has said it expects the census question in England and Wales to align with this standard. [ONS]

Here is how the census works today. You tick a broad group first, and the form then shows you only the options under that group. Tick "Black" and you are offered African, Caribbean or Other Black. "English" appears only if you ticked "White".

Here is what the new design does:

  • The "White" heading becomes "White, any country".
  • The single "English / Welsh / Scottish / Northern Irish / British" box is broken up. English becomes a stand-alone option.
  • Every option is now shown to every respondent, "regardless of which high-level ethnic group they select".
  • Respondents can "select all that apply".
  • There are no rules on who can tick what. The standard "does not include prescriptive guidance for how respondents should answer".

So a respondent ticks "Black" at the first question, then "English" at the second. That is "Black English" in all but name, without a bill and without a vote. [GSS]

And look at how the same document defines ethnicity. The first item on its list of ways to measure it is "where you were born". [GSS] That is the whole argument settled before the public has been asked.

Babarinde told MPs his Bill "takes nothing away from anyone" and that "'White English' stays exactly where it is". [Hansard] The ONS design does the opposite. The combined White box goes, replaced by "White, any country" and a free-standing "English" that anyone can claim.

The timetable: the new question design goes into the 2027 Census Test, and the ONS expects to confirm new tick boxes and publish the emerging standard in Spring 2027. [GSS]

They tried this before, and lost

None of this is new. In 2021 David Lammy complained that he could not record himself as Black English. The ONS answered that "the evidence did not support a change to include Black English in England". [Independent]

In a Freedom of Information response, the ONS went further. Neither "Black English" nor "Asian English" had even been suggested as tick boxes during the consultation for that census. [ONS FOI]

Five years on, the same people are asking the same question. This time the machinery is delivering it.

The counting trap

Here is what they will not tell you: the state has never counted the White English.

In the 2021 census, 81% of people in England ticked a "White" box. That headline hides a lot. It includes 3,585,003 people recorded as "Other White", which covers Polish, Romanian, South African and other White groups. The box most people assume means "us", "White: English, Welsh, Scottish, Northern Irish or British", came to 41,540,791 people, or 73.5% of England. [Census 2021]

That box lumps the English in with the Welsh, the Scots, the Northern Irish and anyone who simply ticks "British". There is no separate figure for the White English. There never has been.

The census also has a separate national identity question, and the 2021 form changed that too. "British" was moved to the top of the list in England. People choosing an "English only" identity in England fell from 60.4% in 2011 to 15.3% in 2021. [ONS] A September 2026 study from University College London tracked the same individuals across both censuses. It found that 53% of people who were English-only in 2011 were British-only by 2021. [UCL] Change the form, change the count.

Look at Birmingham. In 2018, the city council's own community cohesion strategy described Birmingham as "soon to become a majority minority city". [HuffPost] [Birmingham Post] On the council's own census figures, it had already happened, around 2014. The officials were four years behind their own numbers.

Now apply simple logic. The White English are part of the White British figure. So wherever White British is a minority, White English is a minority too, and it got there at the same time or earlier. When? Nobody can say, because nobody has ever been allowed to count it.

And the ONS admits its new design is likely to affect "comparability over time". [GSS] In plain English: the new figures will not line up with the old ones, so the decline cannot be tracked. I believe the White English are already a minority in far more towns and cities than anyone in Whitehall will admit. Under the new form, there will be no number to prove it. In my view, that is not a side effect. It is the point.

Born here is not the same as descended from here

The principle is simple. An inherited ethnic identity and an adopted national identity are two different things, and official statistics must keep them separate.

Where somebody is born, where they live and what passport they hold do not change their ancestry. If two ethnically English parents move to Wales and have a baby, that baby is ethnically English, not ethnically Welsh. If they move to Scotland, the baby is ethnically English, not ethnically Scottish. Birthplace does not change ethnic descent.

"British" is a legal status. The state created it, defines it and hands it out. The government's own website lists six types of British nationality. There is no English nationality among them, and no English passport. [GOV.UK] English was never the state's to give. Now it is trying to give it away.

Babarinde himself conceded the key point in his speech: "English ethnicity is real". [Hansard] He told MPs his mother's side of the family "has been English for as far back as we have traced". So his Bill is not about people like him, with English ancestry. It is about letting people with no English descent at all record English as their ethnicity. His justification: "it is not for politicians to decide who is English. That is for people to say themselves."

If anyone can declare it, the word stops describing a people at all.

The standard reply, which he also made, is that the English were all newcomers once: the Angles, the Danes, the Normans. But those peoples merged over more than a thousand years into one people with a shared descent. That long history is exactly what makes English an ethnicity. A tick box cannot hand it to somebody in a single generation.

Babarinde also told MPs he has received death threats since announcing the Bill. That is disgraceful, and anyone responsible should face the law. It does not change the argument.

The case the Uniparty will not make

Reform frontbenchers Suella Braverman and Robert Jenrick have made the argument that Labour, the Conservatives and the Lib Dems refuse to.

Braverman, Reform's spokesperson for Education, Skills and Equality, was born in England to parents of Indian heritage. In September she wrote:

"Yes, I was born in England, speak English and support the England football team. And indeed, I love England and the English people. But to say I'm English is to take a 'laissez-faire, anything-goes' attitude to ethnicity, citizenship and identity. It also denies me my Asian heritage."Suella Braverman MP, 22 September 2026 [IBTimes]

In the same post she put it in four words: "Englishness is an ethnicity." [Report] On BBC Question Time she said she is British but not English because she is of Asian origin. [Sky News]

Here is an MP of Asian heritage, born and raised in England, drawing that line about herself. That alone demolishes the claim that recognising English ethnicity is about skin colour. It is about descent, and she is honest enough to apply it to herself.

When Kemi Badenoch accused Braverman of "policing other people's identity", Robert Jenrick, Reform's economic spokesman, was blunt: "millions and millions" of people in England are not "culturally or ethnically" English. [GB News]

That is the distinction the census was built to record. It is exactly the distinction the Uniparty and Whitehall are now working to erase.

Watch the spring, not St George's Day

Labour, the Conservatives and the Lib Dems have made their choice. They waved the Bill through without a word of objection. The Prime Minister backed it without reading it. And the civil service had already drawn up the form.

Babarinde's Bill comes back on St George's Day. The real decision lands in Spring 2027, when the ONS publishes its new standard and its new tick boxes. That is the date to watch, because that is when the English, as a people, stop being counted.

— Steve English
