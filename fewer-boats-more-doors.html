Fewer Boats, More Doors

Labour promised to cut immigration. Instead it is rerouting it, and fast-tracking the new arrivals to permanent settlement.

  • The UK Resettlement Scheme reopens immediately, with Afghans and Palestinians prioritised.
  • Arrivals get indefinite leave to remain on day one and can apply for citizenship after five years.
  • Three more sponsorship routes follow: community, university and employer.
  • On the Home Office's own figures, small boat arrivals are still 6% higher than in the year before Labour took office.
PURPOSE: To test the Government's claim that new legal routes will reduce immigration, against its own promises and its own figures.

Editorial approach: This article is written in plain English, following George Orwell's rule: if you can say something simply, do so. It combines investigative reporting with policy analysis and opinion. Satire is used where appropriate to test ideas by applying them consistently. Facts are sourced; opinions are labelled; institutions are not indulged.

Today, at Labour's party conference, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood reopened the UK Resettlement Scheme. It stopped taking new referrals in December 2024. It now restarts with priority for Afghan women and girls, Palestinians with critical medical needs and victims of torture. The Home Office expects about one in four arrivals to come from the Afghan and Palestinian groups.

It does not come alone. From October, charities and community groups can sign up to sponsor refugees, and universities can sponsor refugee students. An employer sponsorship route follows in spring 2027. Numbers start "in the hundreds", with the stated plan to rise to the low thousands.

Ms Mahmood says the system must be "capped and controlled". Labour says safe and legal routes will help drive down illegal crossings. Nigel Farage called it a new push to bring even more migrants into Britain. The figures back him.

A Promise About Numbers, Not Routes

Labour did not win in 2024 by promising to change how people arrive. Voters were told immigration would come down. That meant all of it, not just the part that arrives on a beach in Kent.

What we are now being offered is a relabelling. Fewer people are coming by boat this year, so the Government opens four new doors and calls it fairness. The people are not being refused. They are being redirected.

The Government will point to net migration, which fell to 171,000 in the year to December 2025. But the Migration Observatory warns that the low figures for 2026 and 2027 are likely to be temporary. Emigration is running high as people who arrived during the 2022–23 surge leave again. The Office for National Statistics' own projections have net migration climbing back to around 230,000 a year by 2028. Opening new permanent routes now builds on top of that rebound.

The Same People, Through a Different Door

Look at who crosses the Channel. In Labour's first year in office, Afghans were the single largest nationality arriving by small boat: 6,360 people, up 18% on the year before. In the latest year, Afghans are still in the top five.

Now look at who the new scheme puts at the front of the queue: Afghans. This is not a different population being helped in a different way. It is the same population being given a cleaner way in.

Labour's claim is that the legal route undercuts the smugglers. Madeleine Sumption, director of Oxford's Migration Observatory, which no one would call a Reform outfit, has said there is currently no reason to expect resettlement programmes to significantly change the number of irregular arrivals. So the new route adds to the numbers. It does not replace the boats.

Better Terms Through the Legal Door

Here is the part that should stop readers in their tracks. Refugees who arrive by small boat and win asylum will now have their status reviewed every 30 months, for up to 20 years, under Ms Mahmood's own reforms. That is temporary protection, and it can be withdrawn.

Those who arrive through the resettlement scheme get indefinite leave to remain on arrival, from day one. They can apply for citizenship after five years. Put simply, the resettlement route is permanent from the start.

That rule was introduced by Priti Patel in 2021. Labour has chosen to keep it while hardening the boat route. Both parties have made the same choice: the legal door leads straight to permanent settlement.

Hundreds Now, Thousands Later

Every one of these schemes is built to grow. The resettlement scheme starts in the hundreds and is planned to rise to the low thousands. The Refugee Council has already said hundreds is not enough and is pressing for thousands. The community sponsorship route is modelled on the Ukraine scheme, and the Government's own briefing talks of bringing thousands of refugees from conflict zones.

Four routes, each with its own cap, each designed to scale, each with a lobby pushing for more. When ministers say "capped", read it as "for now".

The Boats: Up, Down, and Still Up

Labour's defence is that the boats are coming down. Here are the Home Office's own official figures, using the same twelve-month window each year, July to June.

Year to June 2024 (the year before Labour)31,493
Year to June 2025 (Labour's first year)43,309  (+38%)
Year to June 2026 (Labour's second year)33,374  (−23%)
Change on the year before Labour+6%

Labour's first year saw arrivals rise by more than a third. Their "success" year took back some of that rise, but not all of it. They are still 6% above what they inherited. The Home Office's own release describes that as "broadly in line" with the year before they took office. That is a generous way to describe going backwards.

The calendar-year picture tells the same story. In 2025, around 41,500 people crossed. That is the second-highest total on record, and it happened on Labour's watch.

There are also fewer boats carrying more people. The average rose to 65 people per boat in the latest year, against seven in 2018. Counting boats flatters the numbers. Counting people does not.

Who Pays

Resettled refugees have access to public funds from arrival. The Home Office funds councils for five years per person. Councils then have to find rented homes, and landlords are paid Local Housing Allowance. These are homes drawn from the same private rented market that local families are competing for.

This is happening while taxes rise across the board, more rises are expected, and the housing crisis shows no sign of easing. The community sponsorship route puts housing on sponsors. When a sponsorship breaks down, the obvious fallback is the local council, and so the taxpayer. Nobody has told the public what happens to that bill in year three, or year six, when the goodwill runs out.

The Only Number That Matters

Labour wants this judged on routes: legal good, illegal bad. The British public was promised something simpler: fewer people coming. On that test, the Government has not even got the boats back to where it found them, and it is now opening four new permanent routes.

A country short of homes does not care which door someone came through. It cares how many came. Labour has not closed a door. It has opened four more.
