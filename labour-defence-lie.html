INVESTIGATIVE OPINION

Labour Is Lying About Defence – And Tomorrow Healey Will Do It Again

Spies are not soldiers. Labour has padded the defence budget with MI5, MI6 and GCHQ money to fake a historic rise in military spending – and it has been caught misleading the public on defence before.

Steve English – Independent British writer and editor · 27 October 2026
  • From 2027-28 the Treasury counts the roughly £5bn-a-year intelligence budget as "defence" spending.
  • NATO's own definition counts armed forces – not spy agencies – and excludes civil defence outright.
  • Former Defence Secretary Sir Ben Wallace has complained to the statistics watchdog, which is considering the case.
  • The same watchdog ruled in August that Labour's £270bn defence "increase" claim was incorrect and misleading.
  • Tomorrow's Budget is expected to repeat the trick.
PURPOSE
To show, from NATO's own rules and the Government's own admissions, that Labour's claimed defence increase is inflated with money that does not belong in it – and to put that on record before the Chancellor stands up tomorrow.

Tomorrow John Healey will stand at the despatch box and tell you Labour is giving the Armed Forces more money than at any time since the Cold War. It is a lie, and Labour knows it is a lie.

Billions of pounds that will never buy a round of ammunition, crew a ship or put a soldier in the field have been quietly shovelled into the defence total: the budgets of MI5, MI6 and GCHQ. Strip them out and the "historic increase" shrinks. Meanwhile, according to the former Defence Secretary who blew the whistle this week, the day-to-day money that keeps our Forces running is being cut in real terms.

Labour has spent so long twisting the truth to the British public that it now thinks it can do the same to our allies and to the men and women who serve. It can't.

The con in numbers

The trick is simple. The Treasury has changed what it calls "NATO-qualifying defence spending". From 2027-28 it will include the Single Intelligence Account – the budget that funds MI5, MI6 and GCHQ – worth around £5bn a year.

Sir Ben Wallace, Defence Secretary from 2019 to 2023, says this lets ministers claim 2.7% of GDP on defence in 2027-28 when only 2.3% is actually spent through the Ministry of Defence. Ministers then compare those padded totals with older, unpadded years to manufacture a dramatic rise.

This is not a one-off. Labour first tried it in June 2025. Rachel Reeves's spending review boasted of defence at 2.6% of GDP, but that figure was built from £71bn of defence spending plus £5.1bn of security and intelligence spending, wrapped together for the first time. Wallace called it a con then. [Evidence] Sixteen months on, Labour has made it permanent.

What NATO actually counts

NATO's definition is not vague. Defence expenditure is money a national government pays specifically to meet the needs of its own armed forces, the forces of other Allies, or the Alliance itself. [Proof]

Bodies outside the armed forces – interior ministry troops, police, coast guards – can be counted only in part, and only for the share of them that is trained in military tactics, equipped as a military force, able to operate under direct military authority, and realistically deployable abroad in support of a military force. Civil defence is excluded outright.

Now apply that to the Security Service at Thames House, the Secret Intelligence Service at Vauxhall Cross and the analysts at GCHQ in Cheltenham. They are not a military force. They are not equipped as one. They do not deploy under military command. They fail every test NATO sets. Intelligence agencies are not mentioned anywhere in the definition – because they are not what the definition is for.

Expect Labour to hide behind one sentence. NATO's page also says spending on other forces paid for by ministries other than the MoD is included. The key word is forces – the military-trained, deployable units described in the test above. A spy is not a soldier because his salary is paid from a different budget line. That sentence does nothing for Labour.

The two-bucket swindle

Here is the part the rest of the press has missed. At The Hague in 2025, every NATO member – Britain included – signed up to a 5% target split into two separate buckets. [Proof]

The first is at least 3.5% of GDP on core defence, measured against NATO's agreed definition. That is the number that shows how much a country is spending on its military.

The second is up to 1.5% of GDP on wider defence- and security-related spending: protecting critical infrastructure, defending networks, civil preparedness and resilience, innovation and the defence industrial base.

If intelligence spending belongs anywhere in NATO's framework, it is in that second bucket. Labour has instead stuffed it into the first – the one that measures the actual military – because that is the number the public, the press and our allies watch. This is not an accounting grey area. They took money from the box where it belongs and put it in the box that flatters them.

They admit it

Labour does not deny the padding. Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds went on television this morning and confirmed the defence budget includes spending on the security and intelligence services. His only defence was that this does not breach NATO rules. [Evidence]

A Government spokesman said the same: that Britain calculates defence spending in line with NATO's definition, which "has not changed". [Evidence] True – the definition has not changed. What has changed is what Labour is stuffing into it.

They don't dispute the lies. They just call it allowed – knowing full well that it is not.

The whistleblower

Sir Ben Wallace has now taken this to the Office for Statistics Regulation, the watchdog arm of the UK Statistics Authority. The watchdog has confirmed it will consider the concerns he raised about how the Government presents defence spending. [Evidence]

In his letter, Wallace says that telling the Forces and the public they are getting more, while their day-to-day funding is cut in real terms, is an insult to them. And he puts it in one line that every serving soldier and veteran will recognise:

"It fools no one inside defence, our allies and our enemies."

Caught before – and told not to do it again

This is the second time in three months that Labour's defence numbers have landed on the statistics watchdog's desk.

On 5 August 2026, the UK Statistics Authority ruled on a complaint about Labour's claim of a "£270bn increase" in defence spending – a claim made by Keir Starmer to a Commons committee, by the Wales Office, and by MoD spokesmen to the press. The Authority found it "incorrect and misleading". The £270bn was simply the total planned defence budget for the whole Parliament, not an increase at all. [Proof]

The MoD then told the watchdog it had briefed its communications staff to make sure misleading lines were not used again. Ten weeks later, here we are.

And notice today's Government statement: Labour is "investing nearly £300bn over the next four years". Another big total, wheeled out in the same breath as the word "increase". They were told. They are doing it anyway.

The rap sheet

The £2.2bn that wasn't for the Forces. In March 2025, Rachel Reeves announced an extra £2.2bn for defence. Most of it was £2bn of extra lending capacity for UK Export Finance – loans to foreign buyers of British defence goods – plus £200m for jobs and regeneration in Barrow-in-Furness. [Evidence] John Healey, then Defence Secretary, hailed it as part of "an extra £5 billion for our Armed Forces". [Evidence] A town centre is not a battalion. A loan to an overseas buyer does not arm a single British soldier.

The £15bn that wasn't new. Wallace says much of the extra £15bn claimed in this year's Defence Investment Plan was not new money at all, but asset sales and cash shuffled between existing MoD programmes.

Cash when it suits, percentages when it suits. Wallace accuses ministers of switching between cash figures and share of GDP depending on which one looks better on the day – and of refusing, despite repeated requests, to publish exactly what they count towards the NATO figure.

If the sums were honest, they would publish the workings.

"Brass neck"

Labour's answer to all this was not a single number. Defence minister Luke Pollard instead accused Wallace of "brass neck", reminding everyone that the Conservatives hollowed out the Armed Forces.

The Tories did hollow out the Forces. Every veteran knows it. But that is not a defence of Labour's figures – it is a change of subject. Attacking the man who caught you does not make your sums add up. If Labour's numbers were straight, Pollard would have published them instead of a playground insult.

Tomorrow's "down payment"

That brings us to the Budget. Healey resigned as Defence Secretary in June, accusing Keir Starmer's government of inadequate ambition on military spending and demanding 3% of GDP by 2030. [Evidence] Now he is Chancellor, and the Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, says tomorrow's Budget will set out how defence reaches 3% by 2030. The Office for Budget Responsibility estimates that needs an extra £17.3bn a year by 2029-30. [Evidence]

Reports say Healey's "down payment" will be billions routed through the National Wealth Fund and the British Business Bank, to be invested in British defence companies – reportedly funded by borrowing. [Evidence]

Ask the obvious question. Is a state investment fund buying stakes in defence firms "spending on the Armed Forces"? Or is it industrial policy with a camouflage net thrown over it? When Healey reads out his percentages tomorrow, watch for three things: whether the spy budget is still in the total, whether investment-fund money is counted as defence, and whether a cash total is once again dressed up as an increase.

An insult to NATO, to the Forces and to you

Our allies can read a spreadsheet. Moscow can read a spreadsheet. The only people Labour is trying to fool are the British public and the men and women it sends into harm's way – and it is failing with them too.

In my opinion, the Labour Party has become so used to twisting the truth to the British public that it now believes it can pull the wool over the eyes of our allies and our Armed Forces without anyone noticing. It has been noticed. It has been reported to the statistics watchdog. And tomorrow, when the Chancellor stands up and claims a historic rise in military spending, every veteran in this country will know exactly how that number was built.

Spies are not soldiers. A loan is not a battalion. A total is not an increase. Labour knows all three. Say it to their faces.
