The Party of Renewal

Frog Soup

If you drop a frog into a pot of boiling water, it will jump straight out to save its life. But if you put it in cold water and very slowly turn up the heat, it will sit there and boil to death without ever moving.

Listen to me closely, People of Britain. You are the frog.

The water is not boiling yet. It is being heated, one gentle degree at a time, right now.

When you voted for them, the water was warm. They seemed refreshing. You thought voting for them would turn the temperature down.

But then. Click. Just 1 degree warmer.

They say it’s about children. They say it’s about safety. They say it’s about protection. They say it’s about diversity.

A little less privacy here. A little more autonomy for them there. A new interpretation of the law. A new restriction. A new legal definition of “phobia”.

Because you have a “stiff upper lip” you adjust. You acclimatise. You tell yourself: it’s just a bit warm, it isn’t unbearable yet.

But the heat keeps rising. And you keep sitting there.

Look at you now. The water is bubbling. Your strength is being cooked out of you. And you are still waiting for them to turn the dial back down.

They won’t. They are hungry. And you are making a very lovely soup.

The only way to survive being boiled is not to beg the Government for mercy. It is to realise your mistake — right now. Find the strength you still have left, and kick them out before it is too late.

Chapter 1

The change came in 2024, carried not by enthusiasm but by arithmetic. The figures had been reviewed and found acceptable. Participation had been modest, unevenly distributed, and largely unmotivated. This was regarded as a strength.

The Party of Renewal assumed authority shortly thereafter.

The transition was described as smooth, orderly, and reassuringly dull. While only a minority had actively endorsed it, a much larger number had declined to object, which amounted to the same thing. Consent, it was noted, did not require enthusiasm. It required only the absence of disruption.

Questions concerning the size of the mandate surfaced briefly and were dismissed just as quickly. Similar questions, it was recalled, had once been raised over a national referendum supported by millions and were at that time considered grave, destabilising, and dangerously irresponsible.

Those standards were no longer applicable. This time, the numbers were different. This time, the numbers were enough. The matter was settled.

The Party of Renewal explained, calmly, that it had been elected to restore seriousness to public life. Seriousness, it clarified, did not depend on turnout, margins, or excitement. These were juvenile metrics, relics of a noisier age. What mattered was authority exercised competently and without drama. By this measure, the outcome was exemplary.

The administration was headed by the First Administrator, Sir TuTier Hughturn, whose suitability for the role was immediately apparent. He possessed no detectable enthusiasm and expressed no personal ambition beyond the continuation of proper procedure. His voice conveyed neither warmth nor threat. It conveyed process.

Sir TuTier Hughturn spoke carefully, often at length, and always without urgency. His statements reassured listeners that nothing abrupt was planned and that nothing unforeseen would be tolerated. He did not promise change. Change had been identified as a source of anxiety. He promised renewal instead, which required no effort on the part of the public beyond compliance.

Observers noted his resemblance to a man made entirely of policy. He stood as one stands when delivering a briefing and spoke as though reading minutes that had already been approved. Many found this comforting. Others found it difficult to remain conscious. Both reactions were considered appropriate.

The absence of celebration following the election was later cited as evidence of national maturity. No crowds gathered. No chants were heard. The nation, it appeared, had grown out of excitement. This was taken as a sign of progress.

I returned that year after time abroad. I had expected to find the country I remembered. Instead, I found arithmetic.

Shortly after the assumption of authority, the ministries were aligned.

The Ministry of Harmony was entrusted with the management of public expression. Its remit extended across platforms, broadcasts, publications, and any environment in which language might be exchanged. The Ministry explained that speech would remain free, provided it remained safe. Content was not removed; it was adjusted. Context was applied where required and withdrawn where distracting. Disagreement, having failed to produce constructive outcomes in the past, was gently reduced. Expression was improved.

The Ministry of National Stability expanded its safeguarding activities. Hostile expressions, misaligned remarks, and statements capable of causing discomfort were recorded for preventative purposes. Reports were welcomed from all sources, including family members, colleagues, and neighbours. Children were encouraged to report parents. Spouses reported spouses. Early intervention remained preferable to escalation. The Ministry insisted that correction was mandatory and remained preferable to punishment.

Economic matters were consolidated within the Ministry of Abundance. Prosperity was described as a process rather than an outcome. Adjustments were made. Support was refined. Where scarcity appeared, it was temporary. Where restraint was required, it was fair. Citizens were reminded that abundance functioned best when expectations were properly managed.

Energy and transition were overseen by the Ministry of Sustainability. The future was described as clean. Costs were described as temporary. Adjustment was universal. Benefit was selective. Citizens were informed that comfort was negotiable. Contribution was not.

Alongside these alignments came the consolidation of Safespeak.

Safespeak was not introduced. It emerged. Older expressions gradually gave way to safer alternatives. Certain words fell into disuse, not through prohibition, but through correction. Statements were revised for clarity.

EchoSphere platform providers updated their standards. Communication improved. Those who persisted in outdated forms were reminded, courteously, of their responsibilities to others.

In the early months, consultations were underway. Protections were under review. Access was being modernised. Guidance would follow in due course.

Participation was encouraged through the appropriate channels. The transition was complete. The ministries were operational. The repair had begun. No further enthusiasm was required.

Chapter 2

The Ministry of Harmony was established to correct a long-standing error: the assumption that people were capable of interpreting events for themselves.

Public discourse, it was observed, had deteriorated under conditions of excessive freedom. Individuals had grown accustomed to forming opinions without instruction and expressing emotions without authorisation. Events were reacted to before they had been properly explained. Images circulated without permission. Conclusions were reached prematurely. Feelings followed.

This state of affairs was no longer tolerable.

The Ministry determined that opinion must be regulated at source. Not because opinion was dangerous in itself, but because unregulated opinion produced unpredictability, and unpredictability produced disorder. Emotional response, in particular, was identified as a destabilising force. People had reacted strongly to matters they did not fully understand, and worse, had reacted before being told how to understand them.

The Ministry of Harmony existed to prevent this.

Broadcasters were informed first. They were reminded that reporting was not the same as interpretation, and that interpretation was not their function.

Their role was to relay authorised understanding in a calm and measured manner. Where events occurred, they were to be reported only once sufficient guidance had been issued. Premature footage was discouraged. Emotional language was corrected. Tone was monitored.

Online EchoSphere platform providers were instructed next.

They were required to adapt immediately and with enthusiasm. Algorithms were adjusted to ensure that approved interpretations reached prominence while unapproved material receded from view. Visibility was no longer determined by relevance or interest, but by compliance. Content that encouraged independent judgement was deprioritised. Content that provoked emotional reaction was restricted. Platform providers that failed to cooperate were reminded that access was a privilege, not a right.

The Ministry was clear: no platform was obliged to continue operating.

Matters relating to public disorder, civil unrest, or social tension were handled with particular care. Images of disturbances were deemed especially hazardous, as they invited interpretation before instruction. Such material was therefore withheld until the correct framing could be applied. In cases where framing was insufficient, access was denied entirely. This prevented panic and discouraged curiosity.

The Ministry emphasised that nothing was being hidden. The public was simply being spared the burden of raw reality.

Independent commentators were addressed directly. They were reminded that free expression did not extend to speculation, contradiction, or unsanctioned analysis. Debate, it was explained, was a primitive habit left over from less stable times. Harmony required agreement, not discussion.

To ensure consistency, the Ministry formalised Safespeak. Safespeak did not restrict language. It replaced it.

Certain words were withdrawn due to their tendency to provoke unauthorised emotion. Others were redefined to ensure alignment. Approved terms were distributed, and deviations corrected. Those who persisted in older forms of speech were informed that such language was no longer appropriate to a mature society.

Statements failing to conform were not banned. They were neutralised. Corrections were appended. Reach was reduced. In persistent cases, the ability to speak publicly was suspended to allow reconsideration. This was described as an opportunity for growth.

Most citizens adjusted quickly. The effort required to hold independent views increased significantly. Many concluded that it was easier to accept the approved position. This was taken as evidence that the system was working.

Expressions of dissent did not disappear entirely, but they became isolated, fragmented, and difficult to sustain. Those who continued to insist on forming their own opinions found themselves increasingly alone. This was not seen as a punishment. It was simply the natural consequence of irresponsibility.

By the end of the year, the Ministry of Harmony was no longer controversial. It was no longer discussed. Its presence was assumed, like infrastructure.

Language flowed more smoothly. Reactions were moderated. Understanding arrived before emotion.

This was considered progress.

Chapter 3

The Ministry of National Stability was responsible for internal security.

Unlike the other ministries, which concerned themselves with systems, language, and resources, the Ministry concerned itself with the individual: their private thoughts, their tone, their associations, and the opinions they formed before instruction had been issued.

This focus was considered necessary.

Experience had shown that harm rarely began with action. It began with thought. Opinions were formed independently. Emotional reactions occurred without authorisation. Jokes were made without warning. These early deviations, if left uncorrected, tended to spread.

The Ministry existed to ensure this did not happen.

It concerned itself not with crime, which was unreliable, but with attitude. Hostility could exist without offence. Disloyalty could exist without words. Silence itself could indicate reluctance to comply. These were not matters for debate.

Reports were encouraged and expected. Citizens were instructed to remain alert to signs of misalignment in others, particularly those close to them. Family

members, colleagues, and neighbours were reminded that reporting was an act of care. Children were encouraged to report parents. Spouses to report spouses. Failure to report suggested sympathy. Sympathy required monitoring.

Reports were logged, retained, and cross-referenced. Most were archived. Some resulted in visits.

Visits were instigated by Ministry of Harmony and conducted by Mindpol officers, typically in the middle of the night. The process was a message. The message was heard by all.

The purpose of the visit was explained calmly. The individual was informed that online expressions had been reported as not adhering to Safespeak, or as Disharmony. Reports came from various sources: EchoSphere platform providers, Syncpol monitoring, Ministry of Harmony observation, or members of the League. The source was logged but not always disclosed.

Some were invited to accompany officers to the station. Acceptance was voluntary. Refusal, however, would result in arrest.

Others were arrested immediately. Cuffs were applied. Cooperation was not requested.

At the station, those not yet arrested received instruction. Clear guidance was given on how to think about particular matters, how to speak about them, and which subjects required silence. Approved formulations were offered.

Compliance would avoid arrest.

Those who accepted instruction were recorded as cooperative and released. Those who refused were arrested.

Those arrested—whether at home or at the station—were processed, bailed, and informed that court would follow. The court would determine whether correction required incarceration.

Old-born Drudge-class organisers and those with large EchoSphere audiences were treated with particular attention. Influence magnified harm. Such individuals were monitored continuously. Where instruction failed, accounts were Quieted. Where Quieting failed, arrest followed.

Families were not exempt from the reporting system. Children were taught in schools that certain views were unsafe and that reporting them was a protective duty. Parents who objected to this instruction were reminded that

safeguarding authority superseded parental rights. The Ministry of National Stability's responsibility to protect citizens extended beyond household privacy.

The Ministry also coordinated the Recognition Grid.

The Recognition Grid operated at public gatherings. Databases contained images of flagged individuals. Matches were automatic. Extraction and arrest was immediate.

Gatherings were recorded. Footage was reviewed. Responses to provocation by the Anti-Disharmony League were identified.

The Ministry worked closely with the Ministry of Harmony to ensure public order events were framed correctly. The Narrative Services filmed selectively. Anti-League provocations were not shown.

Resistance to authority was noted. Unauthorised expressions were logged. The Recognition Grid was linked with The Ledger and EchoSphere, which provided the names and addresses. Mindpol visits followed.

Spontaneous marches were prohibited without notice. Stationary gatherings were permitted but managed. Designated zones were assigned. Time limits were enforced. Distance from buildings was specified. Noise levels were monitored. Compliance was required. Non-compliance resulted in dispersal or arrest.

Protest was not banned. It was controlled.

Attendance at gatherings declined. The Recognition Grid had been observed. Citizens chose discretion. Those who might have spoken in public chose silence instead. Those who might have gathered chose to stay home.

Physical spaces for discussion had been optimised away. Digital spaces were monitored continuously. Thousands of Syncpol officers observed EchoSphere activity without warrant. Reports were filed. Accounts were Quieted. Mindpol visits often followed.

Identification requirements were being extended. Access to the EchoSphere would soon require government-verified identity. Anonymity would cease. Multiple accounts would become impossible. Every expression would be traceable.

The trap closed quietly. Public assembly was managed. Public houses were gone. Online expression was monitored. Soon, it would be identified.

Silence became the safest option. The alternative was Quieting or enforcement.

Over time, behaviour adjusted. Conversations shortened. Humour disappeared. Opinions were withheld entirely. People learned to recognise unsafe thoughts as they formed and suppress them.

This was called self-regulation.

The Ministry of National Stability did not consider itself feared. Fear implied misunderstanding. What it inspired was anticipation. Citizens learned to anticipate what was acceptable and act accordingly.

Visits became less frequent. Not because the Ministry had relaxed, but because most people no longer required reminding.

Stability, properly applied, rendered force unnecessary.

Chapter 4

The Ministry of Abundance was responsible for prosperity. Prosperity, it explained, required contribution.

Prior to assuming power, the Party of Renewal confirmed that stability would continue. Taxes would not rise. Systems would function unchanged.

Immediately after assuming power, the numbers altered.

A deficit was discovered. In truth it was neither new nor discovered. Correction, however, was immediate. Contribution increased.

Charges appeared where none had existed. Obligations expanded. Taxes adjusted, then adjusted again. The adjustments were described as necessary. Necessity was not debated.

Small enterprises calculated the impact. Many closed. This was described as market efficiency. The Ministry stated that consolidation strengthened the economy.

Establishments where citizens gathered socially faced mounting costs. Licensing requirements expanded. Energy bills rose. Taxes rose. One by one, they disappeared. The reduction in public assembly was not mourned by the Party.

Services were refined. Provision narrowed. Efficiency improved, the Ministry explained. Those who required more were advised to adjust expectations.

Meanwhile, resources were allocated visibly elsewhere.

Accommodation was provided for the Swarm People. Not modest provision, but quality establishments. Medical care was stationed. Transportation arranged. Security contracted. Costs accumulated into billions.

This was described as obligation rather than choice. National values, the Ministry observed, must be demonstrated through action.

Citizens who compared allocations were reminded that comparison bred division. Questioning allocation patterns suggested misalignment.

Old-born citizens struggled quietly. Services they had known diminished. Costs they faced rose. Some who had served the nation found themselves without provision. Others worked until capacity failed, then discovered support had been refined away.

Support flowed predictably toward the Swarm People. The allocation was visible, unapologetic, and continuous.

International commitments continued without reduction. Foreign obligations, it was explained, reflected moral standing. Domestic constraint was therefore evidence of principle.

Complaints were noted as evidence of perspective requiring adjustment.

Citizens adapted. Businesses simplified or ceased. Social spaces closed. Expectations recalibrated downward. Comparisons with previous standards were discouraged as unhelpful.

The Ministry of Abundance did not promise comfort. Comfort suggested entitlement. It promised balance.

Balance meant continual rising contribution from the Old-born Drudge-class, directed toward those the Ministry deemed priority.

The logic was not explained. The allocation was not justified. Abundance meant extraction and redirection. The pattern continued.

Chapter 6

The Ministry of Harmony explained that anonymity was outdated.

Anonymity, it noted, belonged to an earlier internet—one defined by disorder, misinformation, and the unregulated exchange of harmful material. In a mature digital environment, access required assurance.

Assurance required identification.

Proof of 18 years of Age verification was mandated. Officially, this concerned adult content. The legislation, however, defined "potentially harmful to minors" more broadly. Protest footage. Riot documentation. Certain news reports. Government publications regarding public disorder.

Access required proof of age. Initially, facial recognition was offered. The technology proved unreliable. The threshold was raised. If the system could not confirm you exceeded the age limit with certainty, identification was required.

Identification meant government-verified documents. Driver's licences. Passports.

The next phase was announced shortly thereafter. EchoSphere access for those under a certain age would be prohibited. Protection of minors, the Ministry explained, required universal verification.

Universal verification meant everyone—regardless of age—would link government identification to their accounts.

This simplified enforcement. When speech was flagged, names and addresses became instantly available. EchoSphere providers, facing substantial penalties for non-compliance, cooperated without resistance.

The warrant process was expedited. Information simply flowed.

Citizens never knew when they had been flagged. Syncpol monitoring was continuous. Public posts were scanned. Private messages were scanned. Definitions of Disharmony were adjusted without announcement. What was permissible one day became actionable the next. Those who attended gatherings did not know whether their presence had been noted online first, or whether their online expressions would be noted at the gathering after.

Uncertainty became constant.

Meanwhile, the Recognition Grid operated at public gatherings. Cameras scanned crowds continuously. Every face was checked against The Ledger. The Ledger contained all persons with outstanding matters.

Matches triggered alerts. Alerts triggered response. Those identified were extracted or arrested depending on circumstance.

The Ministry of National Stability described this as proactive policing. Attendance at Protests declined noticeably. Behaviour adjusted.

The system functioned as designed.

Chapter 7

The ministries released data regularly. The data demonstrated vigilance.

Disharmony offences on the EchoSphere were recorded in increasing numbers. This was described as detection, not prevalence.

Arrests exceeded tens of thousands annually. Charges followed in a fraction of cases. Convictions were rarer still.

This was not considered inefficient. Arrest itself served its function.

Definitions remained flexible. What constituted offence was determined contextually. Grossly offensive material required no fixed standard. Non-trivial harm required no measurable threshold. Intent to cause anxiety required no evidence of intent.

A statement deemed acceptable when posted could become actionable when reviewed. The determination occurred after the fact.

Citizens were informed that context mattered. Context, however, was not defined in advance.

Messages directed at individuals were scrutinised. Messages visible to groups were scrutinised. Messages posted to one's own profile were scrutinised if any group was likely to encounter them.

Likelihood was interpreted broadly.

False information was prohibited where harm was intended. Harm included psychological distress. Distress included anxiety. What the poster knew to be false was inferred rather than proven.

Knowledge, it appeared, could be retrospectively established.

Repeatedly causing upset to any person or group constituted harassment. Repetition was not quantified. Upset was not measured. The boundary between persistent expression and harassment was clarified only through enforcement.

The ministries assured citizens that protections existed for democratic speech and journalistic content. Implementation of these protections remained under review.

Syncpol devoted hundreds of thousands of hours annually to speech-related investigations. This was presented as necessary allocation.

EchoSphere providers cooperated extensively. Thousands of accounts were removed. Thousands more were flagged. Cooperation was mandatory.

Penalties for insufficient removal were substantial. Removal preceded adjudication.

Citizens' advocacy groups noted that arrests had reached approximately thirty per day. They described this as concerning. The ministries described it as proportionate.

Discussion of proportionality did not persist.

Citizens learned that certainty was not available. What was permissible could not be known in advance. Safety lay not in understanding the boundary, but in remaining distant from where it might be.

The numbers continued to rise.

Chapter 8

Not all events were suitable for broadcast.

The Ministry of Harmony explained that selective reporting was not omission but responsibility. Certain facts, though accurate, risked misinterpretation

when presented without guidance. Others, while newsworthy, might provoke disproportionate response.

Stories involving Swarm People accommodation or violent crime, protests and public disorder were reviewed carefully.

Some were reported fully only after being contextualised. A few were omitted entirely.

This was not censorship. This was narrative management. To avoid unsafe responses from the Drudge-class, especially from the Old-born and the Heritage Vanguard.

The Narrative Services followed Ministry of Harmony guidance. Repetition of certain themes was discouraged. Headlines were softened. Images were withheld. Descriptions were adjusted to reduce emotional impact. Certain incidents were misattributed or were mentioned once, briefly, then forgotten. Others were not mentioned at all.

Print media retained a fraction more latitude. This was considered sufficient. The Ministry observed that print reached fewer people and provoked less emotion. Those who sought detail could find it buried in paragraphs on page seven. Most did not look.

The public learned to read between lines. Absence became its own message. If something was not shown, it was not important. If it was not discussed, it had not happened. If it was mentioned only to be dismissed, dismissal was the proper response.

Those who persisted in sharing unapproved narratives on the EchoSphere were redirected to authoritative sources. Accounts were Quieted. Repeat behaviour attracted attention from Syncpol.

Over time, expectations changed.

Citizens stopped asking why certain stories disappeared. They stopped noticing when incidents went unreported. They learned that coverage was guidance, and absence of coverage was reassurance.

If something mattered, it would be explained. If it was not explained, it did not require concern.

This reduced anxiety.

Chapter 9

The intervention began after midnight.

Black-booted officers of the Mindpol arrived without warning. The stairwell was secured. Officers remained on each landing. Neighbours stayed inside. The building fell silent.

The citizen was informed that attendance at the station was required. The matter concerned expressions on the EchoSphere. Attendance was described as voluntary. Refusal would result in arrest.

I watched from a doorway as the citizen was escorted down. The building remained silent. No one intervened. No one looked.

The citizen complied.

Officers followed with the citizen's devices. This was not arrest. Not yet.

At the station, the citizen was placed in an interview room. Syncpol were in attendance.

Their role was not investigation. Their role was correction.

Statements were presented in sequence. Screens displayed posts, replies, remarks made months apart. References to the Swarm People. Questions about allocation patterns. Expression of Old-born sentiment. Pattern was established.

Syncpol did not question content. They questioned thinking. Motivation was examined. Intent was inferred. The citizen was asked to explain the reasoning behind particular expressions. The citizen gave careful answers.

Careful answers were noted as evasive.

Syncpol shifted approach. Context was dismissed. Intent became irrelevant. The concern lay not in any single remark but in the pattern they formed when aggregated. The pattern suggested Heritage Vanguard alignment.

The citizen attempted explanation.

Explanation was noted and set aside.

Syncpol identified tone as problematic. Humour was classified as subversive. Certain subjects were flagged as requiring avoidance. Approved formulations were demonstrated. The citizen was instructed on how to think about particular matters, how to speak about them, and which subjects required silence.

The consequences of non-compliance were stated plainly: arrest on suspicion of Disharmony, charging, prosecution, loss of employment, restriction of EchoSphere access. Duration was unspecified. Charge or release would be determined by alignment.

Agreement was required.

The citizen was presented with a document. The document confirmed understanding of guidance and commitment to alignment. Signature was voluntary. Refusal would result in immediate arrest.

The citizen signed.

Details were entered into the Ledger. The entry did not expire. Status was marked Under Observation.

The citizen was released without charge.

Subsequent expression narrowed. Language softened. Accounts fell quiet. Employment opportunities requiring enhanced background verification became unavailable. The Ledger entry was permanent.

No further instances of disharmony were recorded. This was considered successful correction.

The citizen remained under observation. The system had worked.

Chapter 10

The citizen spoke on subjects designated sensitive. On the EchoSphere and in public gatherings, they discussed allocation disparities. Veterans sleeping rough

while billions funded Swarm People accommodation. Old-born services cut while support flowed elsewhere. Working-class communities altered without consent. Protests met with enforcement while League provocations were facilitated.

They said things plainly. This was not illegal.

It was unregulated.

The citizen's account was flagged for scrutiny. EchoSphere providers were reminded of their obligations. Permission to operate was conditional.

Continued access to the market depended on active alignment with Safespeak standards, proactive suppression of destabilising material, and demonstrable cooperation under penalty.

Penalties were specified. Timeframes were specified.

The EchoSphere providers complied. The citizen's reach did not fall.

It was Quieted.

Posts were published but followers were not notified. Updates appeared but were not highlighted. Safe topics and sensitive topics alike remained invisible. The account existed but no longer functioned.

This process had a name inside the system. Quieting.

The citizen tested the pattern. Posts landed in silence. Nothing travelled. The mechanism was clear but unofficial. No notification was issued. No suspension was declared. No appeal was possible.

Then Syncpol arrived.

The citizen's words were placed on the table. Screens showed statements in sequence. Clips. Captions. Replies months apart. A timeline of emphasis. A diagram of audience reach. References to the Swarm People. Questions about allocation. Expression of Old-born sentiment.

Pattern was established. Intent was dismissed.

Disharmony did not require intent, only effect. The citizen was instructed to sign a statement. The statement was not negotiated.

It affirmed confidence in institutions. It acknowledged prior irresponsibility. It committed the citizen to responsible discourse and to deferring to authorised guidance on sensitive matters. It denied Heritage Vanguard alignment. It promised to avoid language that could be classified as Disharmony.

Refusal was escalation.

Escalation was described calmly: arrest on suspicion of Disharmony, device seizure, bail with conditions restricting speech and prohibiting EchoSphere access, court proceedings, likely imprisonment.
