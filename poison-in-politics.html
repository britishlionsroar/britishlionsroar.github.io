Poison in Politics? Look at Your Own Records First

Labour and the Conservatives say a handful of Reform councillors' words make the whole party unfit for office. Apply the same test to their own people, and to their convictions for rape, child abuse, assault and fraud.

  • An ITV undercover documentary filmed Reform councillors and candidates making racist and sexist remarks. The Prime Minister called it "a poison into our politics".
  • What the cameras caught was words. Labour and Conservative figures, by contrast, have been convicted in court of child sex offences, sexual assault and fraud, and Labour figures of rape.
  • One of the MPs demanding resignations was himself found by Parliament's own watchdog to have bullied a member of his staff.
  • Nigel Farage says the worst offender in the footage spent years as a Conservative councillor before joining Reform.
  • If individual scandals make a party unfit for office, Labour and the Conservatives have supplied the evidence against themselves.
PURPOSE / MISSION: To test the claim that a few individuals' conduct makes an entire party "poison", by applying it consistently to every party, using court records, parliamentary findings and mainstream reporting.

On 15 October ITV News reported the fallout from its undercover investigation, Undercover: A Year in Reform. Reporters spent months alongside Reform candidates and councillors during the May local elections and four by-elections. What they recorded is ugly and indefensible. One Solihull councillor called Asian families "absolute parasites". A Bromley councillor described black children as "animals". A Tameside councillor talked about training young men to go "street-by-street" rounding up migrants. Reform says investigations have begun, and several of those filmed have already left the party.

Nobody should defend those words, and this article does not. But the reaction from Westminster deserves the same scrutiny.

The lectures

Prime Minister Andy Burnham told journalists:

"We've allowed a poison into our politics in recent years and it's time to draw a line on it."

He said he hoped Reform would take "immediate and firm action".

Labour chair Bridget Phillipson went further. She said Farage and his party are "determined to poison our politics" and that the evidence showed "why Nigel Farage and Reform are completely unfit for office".

A Conservative Party spokesman said the footage showed Farage had failed to make Reform ready for government: "These sorry individuals are proof he has failed."

Liam Byrne, Labour MP for Hodge Hill and Solihull North, demanded the councillors resign, saying such views "can have absolutely no place in our public life".

Hold that last name. In April 2022 Parliament's Independent Expert Panel found that Liam Byrne had bullied a member of his own constituency staff, ostracising him for months during the first lockdown, and recommended his suspension from the Commons. The panel called it "a serious breach" arising from "abusing his position of power". He is now lecturing others on standards in public life.

And there is the question of where the worst offender came from. Speaking in Chelmsford, Nigel Farage said the worst of those filmed "was a Conservative councillor for years and years and years who joined us last year". The Conservatives were content to have him on their benches for years. Only now that he wears a Reform rosette has he become proof of a poisoned party.

The consistency test

The argument from Labour and the Conservatives is simple: the words of a handful of councillors and candidates show that Reform as a whole is poison and unfit for office.

Very well. Apply that test to them. Not to words caught on a hidden camera, but to convictions in court and findings by Parliament's own disciplinary bodies. The record below is not exhaustive. Every entry is sourced.

Labour: MPs and peers

  • Lord Nazir Ahmed, former Labour peer: convicted of attempting to rape a young girl and of a serious sexual assault on a boy under 11; jailed for five and a half years (2022).
  • Eric Joyce, former Labour MP: admitted making an indecent image of a child; eight months suspended (2020).
  • Mike Amesbury, MP elected for Labour: pleaded guilty to common assault after punching a man to the ground and hitting him at least five more times; 10 weeks, suspended on appeal (2025).
  • Louise Haigh, Labour Transport Secretary: resigned in 2024 when it emerged she had pleaded guilty in 2014 to an offence after falsely reporting a work phone stolen (a fraud offence; the conviction is spent).
  • Christian Matheson, Labour MP: Parliament's panel found "serious sexual misconduct" towards a junior member of staff and recommended a four-week suspension (2022).
  • Neil Coyle, Labour MP: found to have engaged in drunken abuse, including language with racial overtones; suspended for five days (2023).
  • Liam Byrne, Labour MP: found to have bullied a member of staff; two-day suspension (2022).
  • Diane Abbott, Labour MP: whip suspended over a letter the party called "deeply offensive and wrong" about racism against Jewish, Irish and Traveller people (2023).
  • The Labour Party itself: found by the Equality and Human Rights Commission to have committed unlawful acts of harassment and discrimination over antisemitism, and served with an unlawful act notice (2020).

Labour: councillors, candidates and officials

This is where the "few bad apples" defence collapses. The cases below involve sexual offences, most of them against children, by Labour councillors, candidates and party officials. Several were serving councillors when they were arrested.

  • Liam Temple, Labour councillor and former Mayor of Halton: convicted of inciting a 12-year-old girl to gross indecency; six months (2005).

  • Lee Benson, Labour councillor, Leeds: suspended sentences for child abuse images in 2005 and 2021; a third suspended sentence in 2026 for breaching his court order.
  • Stewart Brown, former Labour councillor, Calderdale, and Mayor of Hebden Royd: possessing and distributing indecent images of children; nine months suspended (2009).
  • Jonathan Phillips, Labour councillor, Walsall: left a memory stick containing child abuse images at a town hall meeting; convicted of making indecent photographs of a child and fined (2009).

  • David Spooner, Labour councillor, North Lincolnshire: two indecent assaults on young men; 12 months (1998).

The Conservatives

  • Charlie Elphicke, former Conservative MP: three counts of sexual assault against two women; two years (2020).
  • Imran Ahmad Khan, Conservative MP: sexually assaulted a teenage boy; 18 months, appeal dismissed (2022).
  • Andrew Griffiths, former Conservative minister: a family court judge found he raped his wife on more than one occasion; findings made public in 2021.
  • Chris Pincher, Conservative Deputy Chief Whip: groped two people at the Carlton Club; eight-week suspension upheld (2023).
  • Peter Bone, Conservative MP: found to have committed "many varied acts of bullying and one act of sexual misconduct", including indecent exposure, against a staff member; six-week suspension (2023).
  • Rob Roberts, Conservative MP: repeated unwanted sexual advances towards a member of staff; six-week suspension (2021).
  • Aaron Bell, Conservative MP: touched a woman inappropriately and without consent in a Commons bar; reprimanded for "brazen and drunken" sexual misconduct (2024).
  • Neil Parish, Conservative MP: admitted watching pornography twice in the Commons chamber; resigned (2022).
  • David Warburton, Conservative MP: admitted taking cocaine; resigned (2023).
  • Owen Paterson, Conservative MP: the Standards Committee found "an egregious case of paid advocacy" for two companies paying him as a consultant (2021).
  • Scott Benton, Conservative MP: told undercover reporters he would breach Commons rules for payment; recommended for a 35-day suspension (2023).
  • Boris Johnson, Conservative Prime Minister: found to have deliberately misled the House of Commons (2023).

Does that make every Labour voter a criminal?

Of course it does not. It does not make every Conservative a sex offender either, or every Labour councillor a danger to children, any more than a handful of filmed councillors makes every Reform member far right. The overwhelming majority of people who stand for any of these parties do so honestly. A party is not its worst member.

That is precisely the point. The same principle has to apply to Reform's members, voters and councillors. Punish wrongdoing. Expel those who disgrace the party. Hold leaders to account for vetting failures, and Reform has clearly failed here. But if a few individuals' words make an entire party "poison" and "unfit for office", then Labour and the Conservatives have spent decades supplying far heavier evidence against themselves. Theirs is not a record of things said in a pub. It is a record of convictions.

Mr Burnham says it is time to draw a line. He might start by drawing it consistently.
