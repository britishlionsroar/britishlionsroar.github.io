They Took Our Money and Ran

43,700 EU graduates living abroad haven't paid back a penny in a year. Labour's answer is to make EU students cheaper. Reform's answer is to shut the door until they pay.

By Steve English • Independent British writer and editor • November 2026
  • 103,400 EU graduates living abroad now owe repayments on British student loans.
  • 43,700 of them (42%) are earning enough to repay, or won't say what they earn, and paid nothing in the last 12 months.
  • £360 million of their loans is in arrears. The government can't even say how fast that figure is growing.
  • EU borrowers owe £6.0 billion on English student loans alone.
  • Labour is preparing to hand universities up to £450 million a year so EU students can pay less.
  • Reform UK will bar graduates in arrears from entering Britain until they pay, cancel the visas of those already here who fall behind, and stop foreign students getting taxpayer-funded loans.
PURPOSE: To set out, using the government's own figures, how much British taxpayers lent to EU students who have since walked away from the debt, what Labour is doing about it, and what Reform UK would do instead.

On 5 October 2026, Labour's education minister Josh MacAlister answered two written questions in Parliament about EU graduates and their British student loans. Between them, the two answers tell you everything you need to know.

There are 103,400 EU borrowers living overseas whose loans are now due for repayment. Of those, 43,700 are earning above the repayment threshold, or have unknown earnings, and have made no repayments at all in the last 12 months. That's 42%. More than four in ten.

The second answer put a price on it. As of 30 August 2026, EU-held student loans that are due for repayment and in arrears totalled £360 million.

Then came the admission that should worry every taxpayer. Asked for the yearly and cumulative figures, the department said it couldn't give a year-by-year breakdown, because the Student Loans Company doesn't keep a historical record of arrears values. Hundreds of millions of pounds overdue, and nobody is tracking how fast it's piling up.

42% of EU graduates abroad who should be repaying paid nothing last year.

How the hole was dug

Before Brexit, EU students at English universities got the same deal as British students. They paid home fees, and the British taxpayer lent them the money to do it.

The catch was always collecting it back. A British graduate working here repays automatically through their payslip. A graduate living abroad has to tell the Student Loans Company what they earn and set up payments themselves. If they stop answering, the SLC puts them in arrears and chases a debt across a border it has no power over.

The government knew what this was costing. When it ended loans for new EU students, its own impact assessment estimated that removing their access to student loans would save taxpayers £2.2 billion. Funding for new EU students without settled or pre-settled status finally stopped in the 2021/22 academic year.

But the bill didn't stop. The SLC's own figures show the EU borrowers' balance on English higher education loans grew from £0.7 billion in 2013–14 to £6.0 billion by 2025–26. Reform puts the total at £6.23 billion, owed by around 172,000 EU borrowers, once Northern Ireland and further education loans are added in.

Labour: “The public expects these debts to be paid”

That's what the minister wrote in his answer: “The public expects these debts to be paid and so do we.” He said the government is investing in the SLC's ability to collect.

Now look at what Labour is actually doing.

Andy Burnham's government is negotiating a reset deal with Brussels, and the EU's main demand is cheaper fees for EU students at British universities. To cover the money universities would lose, ministers are preparing to hand them up to £450 million a year.

So that is Labour's plan. EU graduates abroad already owe £360 million in arrears, and Labour's response is to spend up to another £450 million a year of British taxpayers' money so more EU students can come here cheaper. Labour wants Brussels to like it. The British taxpayer pays for it.

£360 million in arrears. Labour's answer: another £450 million a year to make EU students cheaper.

Reform: you don't come back until you pay

On 21 November 2026, Reform UK's home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf set out the party's answer.

  • Foreign graduates who are behind on their student loans will be barred from entering Britain until they are up to date.
  • Foreign graduates already living here on visas who fall behind will have their visas revoked.
  • The Student Loans Company will be required to share its data with the Home Office, so the border knows who owes.
  • Foreign students will no longer get taxpayer-funded student loans at all.

“Tory and Labour governments have helped foreign nationals scam British taxpayers,” Yusuf said. “This scam has already cost the British taxpayer £360 million. Reform will end the scam. We will stand up for British people.”

The last point isn't new. It's already in Reform's published Apprenticeship Wage Credit policy paper. In the foreword, Nigel Farage and Shadow Education Secretary Suella Braverman write: “We will ban all foreign students from accessing a taxpayer funded loan to pay for their university studies.” The costings section counts on the savings from it to help fund 600,000 apprenticeship starts a year for young British people.

That is the choice in one sentence. Labour spends British money making university cheaper for EU students. Reform stops lending to foreign students and puts the money into training British apprentices.

“It can't be done”? It already has been.

Whoever tells you a border can't be used to collect student debt should look at New Zealand. Since 8 March 2014, New Zealand law has let a court issue an arrest warrant for an overseas-based student loan borrower in default who is about to leave the country after coming back. The court can order them to pay, make them hand over their passport, or keep them in the country until it is sorted.

Reform's plan is gentler than that. Nobody gets arrested. You just don't get back in until you've paid what you owe. A graduate who wants to visit, work or study here again can settle the debt first, like anyone else with a bill.

Pay up or stay out

British graduates can't dodge their loans. It comes out of their wages every month, automatically. For years, EU graduates have been able to dodge theirs by getting on a plane. The government admits 43,700 of them did exactly that last year and doesn't even keep a record of how fast the arrears are growing.

Labour's answer is to make it cheaper for the next lot. Reform's answer is simple: no more taxpayer loans for foreign students, and no entry for those who haven't paid back the last ones.

Apprenticeship Wage Credit: ban foreign students from taxpayer-funded loans

Reform's published policy paper commits to banning all foreign students from taxpayer-funded university loans, with the savings going towards a 30% wage credit for firms taking on apprentices aged 16 to 18, a £2,000 retention bonus, and a target of 600,000 apprenticeship starts a year.
