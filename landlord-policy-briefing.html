Private Landlords: Reform UK Policies and Current Pressures

Policy briefing • 22 November 2026 • Steve English

About this briefing. A focused guide for private residential landlords, primarily in England. It distinguishes current published Reform UK proposals, older commitments requiring reconfirmation, and unresolved questions. Each section links to the relevant fuller document on our site and/or to the original publisher, rather than attempting to reproduce all the underlying material.

At a glance

Reform's October 2026 64-measure programme explicitly proposes repealing the Renters' Rights Act, removing mandatory EPC improvements and making quarterly Making Tax Digital reporting voluntary. The earlier 2024 Contract promised to scrap Section 24. A specific current commitment to abolish the additional-property stamp duty surcharge or reverse the April 2027 property-tax increases has not been verified.

The Renters' Rights Act

Confirmed October 2026 proposal

Reform UK's economic programme, measure 20, states that it would repeal the Renters' Rights Act. Richard Tice separately advocated repeal in February 2026. The Act is currently law, and repeal would require further legislation. Landlords should not assume that existing possession and tenancy procedures have already changed.

EPC C by 2030 and mandatory upgrades

Confirmed October 2026 proposal

Measure 18 proposes to remove mandatory EPC improvements. This matters particularly to private landlords facing the government's 2030 EPC C-or-equivalent policy. Reform's short pledge does not specify which existing certification, safety duties or exemptions it would retain.

Making Tax Digital (MTD)

Confirmed October 2026 proposal

Measure 53 proposes to make quarterly Making Tax Digital reporting voluntary. That is not the same as a pledge to abolish all digital tax records, all returns or the MTD framework. Under current government plans, income-tax MTD is phased in for qualifying individuals according to gross-income thresholds.

Section 24 and mortgage-interest deductions

2024 pledge - current status needs confirmation

Reform UK's 2024 Our Contract with You proposed scrapping Section 24 restrictions, allowing individual landlords once again to deduct mortgage interest and finance costs in calculating rental profits. This remains significant for personally owned buy-to-let property. Because Reform subsequently revised some earlier tax promises, the current status and costing of this pledge need a fresh written confirmation. Company-held properties operate under different tax rules.

Landlord stamp duty

Specific repeal pledge not verified

Purchases of additional residential properties in England and Northern Ireland face higher Stamp Duty Land Tax rates, including a five-percentage-point surcharge. This is distinct from abolishing stamp duty entirely. We have not verified a specific current Reform commitment to abolish that surcharge, change thresholds or remove all SDLT. That is a question to put to the party, rather than a promise to attribute to it.

The rental income-tax increase from April 2027

Current government measure - Reform reversal not verified

Following two successive Budget announcements, the planned increase in the property income-tax rates is a total of four percentage points from 6 April 2027. The announced basic, higher and additional property rates become 24%, 44% and 49%. This is a four-percentage-point rise in tax rates, not a flat 4% rise in every landlord's bill. We have not verified a Reform pledge specifically reversing this increase.

Rental supply, possession and the broader argument

Reform argues that regulation and taxation can make private letting less attractive, discouraging supply and worsening rent pressures. Supporters of the Renters' Rights Act and energy-efficiency requirements argue they strengthen housing security and improve tenants' conditions. Effects on rents, stock and investment depend on local housing conditions and implementation. The policy proposals above should be evaluated on their precise wording, not assumed outcomes.

Six questions worth putting to Reform UK

  1. Will Reform reaffirm and cost its 2024 pledge to reverse Section 24?
  2. Will it reverse the full four-percentage-point property income-tax increase due from April 2027?
  3. Will it remove the additional-property SDLT surcharge, or reform stamp duty in another way?
  4. Exactly which EPC duties and deadlines would be removed and which protections remain?
  5. Would MTD quarterly reporting become optional for every affected landlord?
  6. What possession and tenant protections would replace the Renters' Rights Act?
