“No One Has Had to Fight a War Since”Lisa Nandy Forgot 7,201 British Military Dead to Sell a Labour Party £570m EU Deal

The Culture Secretary used Remembrance Sunday to plug an EU deal that was signed months ago, wrote eighty years of British sacrifice out of history to make the pitch work, and then blamed the Tories for bringing politics into it. Her own department’s press releases prove her wrong.

Steve English · Independent British writer and editor · 9 November 2026
  • In the Government’s official Remembrance Sunday statement, Lisa Nandy said the war generation’s legacy “meant no one in our country has had to fight a war since.”
  • The Ministry of Defence records 7,201 British service personnel killed on operations since 1945 — in Korea, Malaya, Northern Ireland, the Falklands, the Gulf, Iraq and Afghanistan.
  • The same GOV.UK press release carries the Royal British Legion’s tribute to those who served in Bosnia, the Iraq War and the first Gulf War.
  • The Erasmus+ deal was agreed in December 2025 and signed in April 2026. There was nothing new to announce — except the date.
  • £570 million buys one year. The Turing scheme Labour is scrapping had a budget of £78 million.
PURPOSE: To show how a Labour Cabinet minister used the nation’s day of remembrance to sell an EU deal, wrote the post-war dead out of history to make the sales line work, and then denied doing what her own department had put in writing.

At eleven o’clock on Sunday the country fell silent for its war dead. The night before, the Department for Culture, Media and Sport had already told the press what that silence was for.

“As we mark Remembrance Sunday,” the department announced, “Erasmus+ will give a new generation the chance to build connections across Europe.” The Culture Secretary, Lisa Nandy, put her name to it: “As we remember the immense courage of those who served our country, we honour them by building the best opportunities for the next generation through closeness and collaboration with our allies.”

Her department went further. It said she “believes that we owe it to our brave veterans to repair these relationships with our friends in Europe” by rejoining the EU’s student exchange programme.

That was the pitch. Honour the dead. Pay Brussels.

“No one has had to fight a war since”

On Sunday morning the Government published its official Remembrance Sunday press release, issued in the names of the Prime Minister, the Defence Secretary and the Culture Secretary. In it, Lisa Nandy wrote:

“They went to war to give us 80 years of peace … Our future generations must know the significance of those who served in our national story, that they forged a path for friendship and cooperation that meant no one in our country has had to fight a war since.”

No one. Tell that to the families.

The Ministry of Defence keeps the count. Its latest official statistics, published in March 2026, record 7,201 UK armed forces personnel who died as a result of operations between 3 September 1945 and 28 February 2026. Among them:

  • Malaya: 1,442
  • Northern Ireland: 1,441
  • Korea: 1,129
  • Afghanistan: 457
  • Falklands: 237
  • Iraq (Operation Telic): 178
  • First Gulf War: 45

Those are not footnotes to “80 years of peace.” They are names on the memorials people stood beside on Sunday morning, while the Culture Secretary’s statement sat on the Government website.

Col Richard Kemp, the former commander of British forces in Afghanistan — where 457 of them died — told the Telegraph: “On Remembrance Sunday, it is not about Erasmus or anything like that – it is all about remembering and commemorating the people who made the supreme sacrifice.”

Robert Jenrick, Reform UK’s Treasury spokesman, put it plainly: “While she erases their service, the country will never forget their bravery.”

Contradicted on her own press release

Here is the part Labour cannot explain away. The same GOV.UK press release that carries Nandy’s “no war since” line also carries a statement from Mark Atkinson, Director General of the Royal British Legion:

“This year we have paid tribute to those who served in more recent conflicts, including the challenging operations in Bosnia, the Iraq War and the first Gulf War, and recognised the families who continue to live with the impact of that service.”

One page. The Legion honours the men and women who served in Bosnia, Iraq and the Gulf. A few paragraphs away, the Culture Secretary says nobody in this country has had to fight. Either nobody in her department read the page before it went out, or somebody did and did not care.

Same press release. The Legion remembers Iraq, Bosnia and the Gulf. The Culture Secretary says no one has had to fight a war since 1945.

Nothing new to announce — except the date

Labour did not negotiate anything this weekend. The UK and the EU agreed the deal to rejoin Erasmus+ in December 2025. The legal texts were signed in April 2026.

What was “new” on Saturday night was a start month — January — and up to £400 million channelled through the British Council to schools, youth groups and sports teams. Neither needed Remembrance Sunday. That release could have gone out on any of the other 364 days of the year. It went out on the one day when the whole country gathers at its war memorials.

Then Nandy sat down with Laura Kuenssberg on the BBC and said: “I am not choosing today or any day actually to promote a deal with the EU.” Challenged on her department’s wording, she added: “I don’t think we’re promoting a deal with the EU at all.”

Her department’s own words were: “As we mark Remembrance Sunday, Erasmus+ will give a new generation the chance to build connections across Europe.”

The Conservative Party chairman, Kevin Hollinrake, summed it up: “Lisa Nandy can deny it all she likes, but her own department’s press release tells a very different story. Her denials are frankly disingenuous and fly in the face of the facts. Our veterans’ sacrifice is not a prop for Labour’s EU agenda.”

Caught out — so blame the Tories

Having tied an EU scheme to the war dead, Nandy went on Sky News and accused her critics of politicising the day: “It’s Remembrance Sunday. It’s not a time for politicking, and I’m actually quite horrified that the chairman of the Conservative Party has chosen to do that. They are the only party that is talking about Brexit and the European Union today.”

The only party talking about the European Union on Remembrance Sunday? Her own department sent out a press release about it on Saturday night.

The veterans saw straight through it. Col Kemp told the Telegraph:

“This is incredibly hypocritical from Lisa Nandy – but it’s entirely in keeping with what you would expect from this Government, who don’t seem to really care about our Armed Forces … This has backfired and, rather than holding her hands up and apologising, Nandy has decided to blame her opponents and claim they have politicised an issue entirely of her own making.”

Lord West, the former First Sea Lord — and a former Labour security minister — said her comments were “deeply unimpressive” because “she was the one who politicised it.” He went on: “Erasmus has got nothing to do with the military, and effectively saying ‘this is why our young men died in these wars’ is complete and utter twaddle.”

Maj Gen Timothy Cross CBE accused her of using the day for “political point scoring.” Peter Sharples, chief executive of the Veterans Institute, said people should “just honour the fallen” on Remembrance Sunday rather than discuss “party political matters.” Nigel Farage called it “very, very poor taste.”

For King, Queen and country — not for Brussels

Nandy did not back down. On the BBC she reached for Churchill: “It was Winston Churchill after the Second World War who talked about extending the hand of friendship across Europe and making sure that we honoured the legacy of that generation.” She said that after Brexit “we closed off opportunities for young people from this country to understand not just the impact that generation made here in the UK, but the way in which they went off and fought for the freedom of people across Europe.”

The men who landed in Normandy did not fight for a youth exchange programme. They fought for King and country, and to free nations that Hitler had overrun — free to govern themselves again, as we were. The European Union did not exist until 1993. Churchill’s hand of friendship did not come with an annual membership fee. And a schoolchild from Wigan who wants to understand what their great-grandparents did can stand at the war graves in France and Belgium today. No £570 million cheque required.

And the wars Nandy says nobody had to fight? Northern Ireland, the Falklands, the Gulf, Bosnia, Iraq, Afghanistan — every one was fought under Queen Elizabeth II. The men and women she wrote out of history went for Queen and country. They did not go for a closer relationship with the EU, and the country owes them better than a press release that pretends they were never sent.

The £570 million question

Hollinrake called it “a £570m cheque to Brussels.” The numbers back him up.

  • £570 million buys one year. The deal covers only the 2027–28 academic year. What Britain pays from 2028 has not been agreed.
  • The “saving” is not a saving. Nandy’s department claims a 30 per cent discount will “save taxpayers around £240m.” A discount on a bill you did not have to run up is not a saving. It is a smaller cost.
  • The scheme it replaces cost a fraction. The Government set the Turing scheme’s budget at £78 million for 2025–26. Year one of Erasmus+ costs more than seven times that. Turing is being shut down.
  • The bill is set to grow. Brussels plans to expand the scheme from 2028. The Telegraph has reported that Britain’s fee could reach £1.25 billion a year.

Farage described Erasmus as a “terrible scheme” and “wildly expensive” next to Turing. And this is only the start: talks on a new EU youth mobility scheme are running ahead of a UK–EU summit on 20 November.

Opinion

The verdict

This was not a slip of the tongue in a live interview. It was a written statement, drafted, signed off and published on the Government’s own website. It wiped 7,201 British dead out of the record because the sales line — eighty years of peace, thanks to Europe — did not work with them in it.

Then, when the country noticed, the Culture Secretary denied what her department had written and accused everyone else of playing politics. That is not a misjudgement. It is contempt — for the dead, for the families, and for the public’s ability to read a press release.

What Labour must do now

  1. Lisa Nandy must withdraw the statement and apologise to the families of every serviceman and woman killed on operations since 1945.
  2. Correct the GOV.UK Remembrance Sunday press release.
  3. Publish who in the Department for Culture, Media and Sport drafted, approved and timed the Saturday-night Erasmus+ release.
  4. Publish the full expected cost of Erasmus+ from 2028 before any further commitment is made.

Remembrance Sunday belongs to the dead. Not to a minister’s press office, and not to Brussels.
