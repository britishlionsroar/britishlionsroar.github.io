SCRAPPED: The UN’s Climate Doomsday Was Never Going to Happen

For a decade, the most-quoted climate forecast in the UN’s reports told the world it was heading for 4.6°C and catastrophe. Now the scientists who write the UN’s climate scenarios admit it was “implausible”. They were told it was “exceptionally unlikely” back in 2017. Children cried. Pensioners glued themselves to trains. Britain built its plans on it.

Steve English · Independent British writer and editor · 12 November 2026
  • The UN’s most-quoted climate forecast — 4.6°C of warming by 2100 — has been scrapped by the scientists who write the scenarios for the UN’s next climate report. Their word for it: “implausible”.
  • Scientists warned in 2017 that it was “exceptionally unlikely”. It stayed in use for almost ten years.
  • It depended on the world burning five times more coal. Coal use is at a record high — and it still comes nowhere close.
  • More than half of young people surveyed worldwide said they believed “humanity is doomed”.
  • Britain’s official climate projections still use it as their high case.
PURPOSE: To expose how the most frightening climate forecast of the last decade was known to be unrealistic for years, how it was sold to the public as the road we were already on, and why every British plan built on it must now be reopened.

October 2019. Canning Town station, east London. Two Extinction Rebellion protesters climb onto the roof of a packed rush-hour Tube train and unfurl a banner: “Business as usual = death”. At Shadwell, an 83-year-old protester glues his hand to the side of a train. Commuters trying to get to work drag the men off the roof.

Two years later, the largest survey of its kind questioned 10,000 young people aged 16 to 25 in ten countries, including Britain. Three-quarters said the future was frightening. More than half — 56 per cent — said they thought humanity was doomed. Nearly half said their fear was affecting their daily lives.

“Business as usual = death.” That phrase did not come from nowhere. For years, “business as usual” was the label hung on one climate scenario above all others — RCP8.5, the forecast of a 4.6°C world by 2100.

That forecast has now been scrapped.

What was scrapped

On 7 April 2026, the journal Geoscientific Model Development published the scenarios for the next round of global climate modelling. They were written by 44 scientists, led by Detlef van Vuuren of the PBL Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency. These are the scenarios the UN’s Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change will build its next report on.

The verdict on the old top-end forecast, SSP5-8.5, is in black and white. Its emission levels have “become implausible”.

Implausible. Not “less likely”. Not “at the edge of the range”. Implausible.

Fox Business put it bluntly on screen: “UN withdraws worst-case climate conclusion.” The fact-checkers rushed out to say the UN itself had not issued a statement. True. The scientists who write the UN’s scenarios did the withdrawing for it.

Not a fringe forecast — the UN’s favourite

This was not some obscure footnote. The Competitive Enterprise Institute counted the references in the UN’s last major climate report and found RCP8.5 and SSP5-8.5 cited 1,359 times — more than any other scenario.

It was the number behind a decade of the most frightening climate headlines and the “last chance to save the planet” summits.

The real numbers

Carbon Brief — no friend of climate sceptics — set out the scale in September. The top forecasts in the last three UN assessment cycles all produced between 4.6°C and 4.9°C of warming by 2100. The new top forecast gives 3.3°C.

The carbon behind it has been halved. The old forecast assumed about 7,600 billion tonnes of CO2 between 2024 and 2100. The new one assumes about 3,820 billion.

The doomsday ceiling has dropped by 1.3°C. That is not a correction. That is a different planet.

They were told for almost ten years

None of this is new. It was known — and ignored.

In 2017, Justin Ritchie and Hadi Dowlatabadi of the University of British Columbia published a paper in the journal Energy titled “Why do climate change scenarios return to coal?” Their finding: the forecast rested on the world swinging back to coal on a colossal scale. They called RCP8.5 and the other so-called “business-as-usual” scenarios “exceptionally unlikely”.

In 2020, two mainstream climate scientists, Zeke Hausfather and Glen Peters, wrote in Nature that everyone — scientists, communicators and policymakers — must “stop presenting the worst-case scenario as the most likely”. They warned it “becomes increasingly implausible with every passing year”.

The research machine kept running anyway. Roger Pielke Jr, who has written on this with Ritchie, counts almost 10,000 studies published in 2025 alone still using scenarios he says were already falsified.

Six years after they were told to stop, the frightening numbers were still pouring out.

Five times the coal

Strip it down and the doomsday forecast was a bet on coal.

The storyline behind SSP5-8.5 needed coal use per person to rise around six-fold by 2100, against a long-term record that had stayed flat. Even the scientists who defend its past use admit RCP8.5 assumed a five-fold increase in coal use — including turning coal into oil to run cars.

Now look at the real world. The International Energy Agency reports that world coal demand hit 8.85 billion tonnes in 2025 — an all-time record. The IEA expects it to level off, not collapse. All the cuts in Britain and Europe have not dented the global total.

So the defenders’ excuse — that green policy and cheap solar panels changed the path — falls apart. Coal was not beaten. It is at its highest ever. And the doomsday forecast still needed about five times more of it.

The defenders’ own sums give the game away. Hausfather, Peters and Piers Forster credit policy and technology with knocking about 0.7°C off expected warming — out of a 1.7°C gap between RCP8.5 and today’s central forecast. In their own words, RCP8.5 was never a likely outcome even in a world that did nothing about climate change.

Most of the doomsday gap had nothing to do with Net Zero. The forecast was simply never real.

Britain built its plans on it

This did not stay in the journals. It reached Kent.

On 11 March 2019, a Met Office adviser told Kent County Council’s Flood Risk and Water Management Committee that RCP8.5 represented the “reasonable worst-case scenario”.

The Met Office’s UK Climate Projections, UKCP18 — led by Defra, with the Environment Agency and the Met Office as delivery partners, and used by councils such as Kent for planning — use RCP8.5 as their high case. A Met Office briefing from September 2025 still spells it out: high emission scenario = RCP8.5.

A 2024 report for the UK Climate Resilience Programme found British organisations treating RCP8.5 as anything from “business as usual” to “worst case” — and often wrongly taking it to mean a 4°C world.

So British flood plans, adaptation reports and risk registers have been resting on a forecast its own designers now call implausible.

And 1.5°C is dead too

The same paper quietly binned the other end of the range. The old best case, which held warming close to 1.5°C, is gone. The paper now says that “some overshoot of the 1.5 °C seems unavoidable”. Van Vuuren told AFP the team no longer finds it plausible to stay at 1.5°C with only limited overshoot.

Net Zero by 2050 was sold to the British public on 1.5°C. The scientists who write the UN’s scenarios now say that target will be missed.

Opinion

Who answers for this?

A generation of children was told the world was ending. Pensioners glued themselves to trains. Governments — Britain’s above all — loaded the cost of the fight onto household bills and businesses, in the name of a crisis whose most frightening version was known to be unrealistic years before it was dropped.

The scientists who now call it implausible were not the ones who sold it as “business as usual”. But nobody in authority stopped the selling. Not the UN. Not the Met Office. Not our ministers.

  • The Met Office must issue revised UK projections that drop RCP8.5 as the high case, and say plainly which official advice relied on it.
  • The Environment Agency and councils — Kent included — must review every flood allowance, planning condition and adaptation budget set against RCP8.5.
  • The Climate Change Committee must tell Parliament which of its advice leaned on RCP8.5 or SSP5-8.5.
  • Ministers and every public body must stop presenting this forecast to the public — and to children — as the road we are on.

The party that says: scrap it

One party has already put the answer in writing. Reform UK’s published policies would tear up the Net Zero project that was sold to the British public on fear.

  • Cut energy bills by £250 within 100 days. Reform’s “Cut your Energy Bills” pledge would remove all government-imposed policy costs from energy bills, end the charges on generators for their emissions, and move renewable subsidies off household bills while unfair contracts are unwound.
  • A “bonfire of the Net Zero vanities”. Reform’s Economic Rescue Plan would scrap the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, the Electric Car Grant, the Zero-Emissions Vehicle mandate, Carbon Capture and Storage spending and Great British Energy (except nuclear), along with the Net Zero spending hidden across schools, the NHS and Whitehall. Its own verdict on the project: it “never had a hope of measurably reducing global temperatures”.
  • £8 billion to £10.7 billion a year saved. The plan’s fiscal tables score scrapping Net Zero subsidies at £8.18 billion in its first year, rising to £10.67 billion.
  • Unlock the North Sea and cheap power. Approve Rosebank, restart oil and gas licensing, abolish the Energy Profits Levy, strip the Net Zero duty from the North Sea regulator, abolish the Emissions Trading Scheme and the carbon border tax, and fast-track new nuclear — all set out in the same plan.
